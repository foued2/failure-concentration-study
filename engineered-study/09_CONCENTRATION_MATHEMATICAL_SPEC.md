# Concentration hypothesis: mathematical specification and worked examples

**Status:** Authorized offline mathematical work. This file specifies the target, a latent-defect representation, identification requirements, and prespecified failure criteria. It includes deterministic arithmetic examples only.

**Not:** A simulation, a generated-solver study, evidence about real solvers, or authorization to reopen Gate 1. No solver generation, model/API call, random sampling, bank change, or launch is part of this artifact.

## 1. Scientific question

The proposed predictive hypothesis is:

> Among solvers with the same diagnostic failure count, a higher concentration of diagnostic failures predicts a higher score on separate evaluation inputs, beyond the baseline information already available (at minimum, total diagnostic failures and generator identity).

This is a predictive claim, not a claim that the numerical statistic \(C\) causes solver correctness. \(C\) is calculated from diagnostic outcomes. Any predictive relationship must arise because the diagnostic pattern carries information about the solver's underlying weaknesses and how those weaknesses extend to evaluation inputs.

## 2. Observed quantities and scoring

There are \(J=6\) diagnostic families. Each family has five diagnostic inputs. The evaluation-family counts for the length-seven parenthesis task are

\[
N=(N_1,\ldots,N_6)=(9,9,23,23,31,3), \qquad \sum_{j=1}^6 N_j=98.
\]

For solver \(i\), let \(Y_{ix}=1\) if the solver fails input \(x\), and \(0\) otherwise. Let \(D_j\) be diagnostic inputs in family \(j\), and \(V_j\) be evaluation inputs in family \(j\). Define

\[
f_{ij}=\sum_{x\in D_j}Y_{ix}, \qquad e_{ij}=\sum_{x\in V_j}Y_{ix}.
\]

Thus \(f_{ij}\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), and \(e_{ij}\in\{0,\ldots,N_j\}\). The diagnostic failure vector, total count, and concentration are

\[
F_i=(f_{i1},\ldots,f_{i6}),\qquad k_i=\sum_j f_{ij},
\qquad C_i=\frac{\max_j f_{ij}}{k_i}\quad(k_i>0).
\]

**Zero failures:** \(C_i\) is undefined when \(k_i=0\), not zero. The all-correct diagnostic cases must be reported as their own stratum. They can contribute to a broad score-prediction task, but they must not be treated as observations with “zero concentration” in a fixed-\(k>0\) test.

The domain-weighted evaluation score is

\[
S_{\mathrm{domain},i}=1-\frac{\sum_j e_{ij}}{98}.
\]

The equal-family score is

\[
S_{\mathrm{family},i}=1-\frac{1}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{6}\frac{e_{ij}}{N_j}.
\]

More generally, a family-weighted score can be written as
\(S_{w,i}=1-\sum_j w_j e_{ij}/N_j\), where \(w_j\geq0\) and
\(\sum_jw_j=1\). The domain-weighted version has \(w_j=N_j/98\); the equal-family version has \(w_j=1/6\).

For the existing engineered design, domain-weighted score remains the proposed primary measure and equal-family score is the required secondary check. A claim intended to generalize beyond a specific scoring convention must not rely on a direction that reverses under this secondary check.

## 3. A latent-defect representation

Let solver \(i\) have a set of latent defect mechanisms indexed by \(m=1,\ldots,M_i\). Define:

- \(Z_{im}\in\{0,1\}\): whether defect mechanism \(m\) is present in solver \(i\);
- \(A_{imx}\in\{0,1\}\): whether mechanism \(m\), when present, makes input \(x\) fail.

One deterministic representation of the solver's failure indicator is

\[
Y_{ix}=1-\prod_{m=1}^{M_i}(1-Z_{im}A_{imx}).
\]

The formula says an input fails if at least one present defect causes a failure on it. It is a bookkeeping model, not an assertion that every real solver has a uniquely separable list of defects. Interacting defects, masked defects, runtime faults, and stochastic outputs would require extensions.

Diagnostic failures \(F_i\) and evaluation failures \(E_i=(e_{i1},\ldots,e_{i6})\) are both generated by the defect footprints \(A_{imx}\). The missing relationship is the **transport or leakage relationship** between those footprints on the diagnostic set and their footprints on the evaluation set.

Write that relationship abstractly as

\[
P(E_i\mid F_i,M_i,G_i,T,\mathcal P)=\Psi,
\]

where \(M_i\) denotes defect structure, \(G_i\) generator identity, \(T\) the task/domain, and \(\mathcal P\) the diagnostic-family partition. Here \(\Psi\) is not a known scalar; it stands for the conditional distribution linking diagnostic footprint to evaluation damage. The relationship may depend on the number, type, scope, interaction, and overlap of defects.

This is the central unresolved quantity. Knowing \(F_i\), \(k_i\), \(C_i\), family sizes, and scoring weights does **not** mathematically determine \(E_i\) or \(S_i\). Without restrictions on \(\Psi\), the same diagnostic record can coexist with very different evaluation results. A predictive theory needs a defensible reason why this link is stable enough for \(C\) to carry information.

## 4. Primary estimand: predictive gain, not a coefficient alone

Let \(Z_i\) denote prespecified context covariates and design information, with generator identity \(G_i\) included when the target population contains multiple generators. Compare two prediction procedures:

\[
\mathcal M_0: \hat m_0(k_i,G_i,Z_i)
\qquad
\mathcal M_C: \hat m_C(k_i,G_i,Z_i,C_i).
\]

The target is the held-out reduction in squared prediction error,

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{pred}}
=\mathbb E\!\left[
(S_i-\hat m_0^{(-i)})^2
-
(S_i-\hat m_C^{(-i)})^2
\right].
\]

The superscript \((-i)\) indicates that the prediction for solver \(i\) is generated without training on that solver or its duplicate/near-duplicate mechanism cluster. Positive \(\Delta_{\mathrm{pred}}\) means adding \(C\) improves prediction on held-out solvers. Zero means no improvement; negative means it worsens prediction.

This estimand is intentionally predictive. A positive fitted coefficient for \(C\) in the same data used to fit the model is not sufficient. Predictive comparisons must use identical outer hold-out cases, with tuning and feature selection confined to training folds. Duplicate solver replicas and mechanically equivalent predicates must be kept in the same fold to avoid leakage.

The baseline requested by the original claim is \(k\) plus generator identity and prespecified context. Family/carrier identity is a competing explanation and must be examined in a separately prespecified adjusted comparison wherever the data support it. If adding family controls makes \(C\) perfectly collinear or removes all common support, the conditional effect is not identified in that population; a regression package returning a number would not repair this.

Two related but different questions must not be conflated:

1. **Does \(C\) predict \(S\) beyond \(k\) and the declared baseline?**
2. **Does the full vector \(F\) predict \(S\) better than the summary \(C\)?**

Since \(C\) is a deterministic function of \(F\), an ideal predictor with unlimited data and an unrestricted function class can reproduce any predictor based on \(C\) when given \(F\). Therefore the full vector cannot contain less information in principle. In a finite dataset, however, a model using all vector components can predict worse because it estimates more parameters and may overfit. The empirical comparison must use the same held-out cases and fair, nested model-selection procedures.

## 5. Deterministic worked arithmetic

These examples are constructed to expose the equations and possible confounding. They are **not** simulated solvers and are **not** observations supporting the theory.

### 5.1 Same \(k\), different \(C\)

The following profiles are all valid because each diagnostic family has five inputs.

| Diagnostic profile \(F\) | \(k\) | \(C\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((5,0,0,0,0,0)\) | 5 | 1.0 |
| \((1,1,1,1,1,0)\) | 5 | 0.2 |
| \((2,2,1,0,0,0)\) | 5 | 0.4 |
| \((2,1,1,1,0,0)\) | 5 | 0.4 |

This demonstrates mathematical attainability of multiple concentrations at fixed \(k=5\). It does **not** show that real or independently constructed solvers from the target population can realize these profiles without changing defect identity.

The last two rows also show information discarded by \(C\): the vectors differ even though both have \(k=5\) and \(C=0.4\).

### 5.2 Family size can change the raw score while \(C\) stays at 1

Suppose a defect fails all five diagnostics in exactly one family and, as an illustrative rule, every evaluation input in that same family also fails. Then \(k=5\) and \(C=1\) whichever family carries the defect. The domain-weighted score becomes \(1-N_j/98\).

| Evaluation family size \(N_j\) | Domain-weighted score \(1-N_j/98\) | Equal-family score |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 9 | \(89/98=0.908163\) | \(5/6=0.833333\) |
| 23 | \(75/98=0.765306\) | \(5/6=0.833333\) |
| 31 | \(67/98=0.683673\) | \(5/6=0.833333\) |
| 3 | \(95/98=0.969388\) | \(5/6=0.833333\) |

The raw-score range comes solely from the evaluation-family size under this illustrative defect rule. Equal-family weighting removes that size effect in this example, but it does not remove all differences in defect scope or identity. Thus scoring weights can create or conceal an apparent concentration contrast.

### 5.3 Same diagnostic record, different possible evaluation outcomes

Fix \(F=(5,0,0,0,0,0)\), so \(k=5\) and \(C=1\).

- If \(E=(0,0,0,0,0,0)\), then \(S_{\mathrm{domain}}=1\).
- If \(E=(9,0,0,0,0,0)\), then \(S_{\mathrm{domain}}=89/98=0.908163\).
- If \(E=N=(9,9,23,23,31,3)\), then \(S_{\mathrm{domain}}=0\).

These are algebraically valid outcome vectors. They are not asserted to be equally likely under any realistic defect mechanism. The point is that the diagnostic vector alone does not specify which evaluation outcome occurs. A mechanism model or empirical evidence must constrain the possible diagnostic-to-evaluation links.

### 5.4 What a mathematical example can and cannot prove

A rule that explicitly sets expected score to rise with \(C\) will produce a positive relationship by construction. A rule making expected score independent of \(C\) will produce no relationship; a rule linking \(C\) to wider evaluation leakage can produce a negative one. All three can be specified mathematically. Choosing the first rule is not evidence for the theory.

Therefore an offline model should examine competing, clearly labelled assumptions and determine which assumptions are sufficient for the prediction, which observable comparisons can distinguish them, and which parameters remain unidentified. The assumptions must not be selected because they produce a preferred sign.

## 6. Identification and overlap requirements

Before treating data as capable of testing the hypothesis, all applicable conditions below must hold.

1. **Defined concentration.** The primary fixed-\(k\) analysis is restricted to \(k>0\). \(k=0\) is handled separately.
2. **Within-\(k\) variation.** At least one prespecified \(k\) stratum must contain different \(C\) levels. A concentration value that is a deterministic function of \(k\) cannot be separated from \(k\).
3. **Mechanism overlap.** Each compared concentration level must be represented by more than one independent defect/mechanism cluster. Equivalent predicates and replicas do not count as independent mechanisms.
4. **Generator overlap.** If the intended claim adjusts for generator identity, the compared concentration levels must overlap within the generators to which the adjusted claim applies. If the claim is about generalization to new generators, evaluation must hold out entire generators, and enough independent generators must exist to make that estimable.
5. **Family/carrier separability.** Concentration must not be perfectly determined by a family/carrier label or a prespecified mechanism label. The design matrix for the declared comparison must have full column rank, and the relevant fixed-\(k\) contrasts must have support after the declared controls. Full rank is necessary but not sufficient for reliable estimation.
6. **Distinct outcomes.** A matched-profile comparison intended to demonstrate mechanism diversity must differ in full-domain failure footprint or another independently justified aspect of mechanism. Different candidate names alone do not establish different mechanisms.
7. **Evaluation separation.** Diagnostic and evaluation inputs must be disjoint, and evaluation membership/outcomes must not be used to construct solver candidates or choose thresholds. The prior audit disclosed that some text-statistic threshold candidates were derived from feature values observed in the diagnostic bank; that history must remain visible.
8. **Score and partition fixed in advance.** The primary scoring rule, secondary score, family definitions, alternative defensible partitions, and all analysis comparisons must be specified before a confirmatory outcome is examined.

The existence of attainable \((k,C)\) cells is not enough. Construction and overlap in the intended population are empirical feasibility questions.

## 7. Competing comparisons

The analysis plan must distinguish these comparisons rather than interpret every association as a concentration effect.

- **Core predictive comparison:** \(\mathcal M_0\) versus \(\mathcal M_C\), where the latter adds \(C\) beyond \(k\) and the prespecified baseline.
- **Family/carrier alternative:** assess whether the relationship remains when the prespecified, estimable family/carrier controls are added. If common support is lost, report non-identification rather than an adjusted effect.
- **Full-vector comparison:** compare \(C\) against \(F\) as predictive feature representations on the same held-out solvers. Report both out-of-sample loss and model complexity; do not claim \(C\) contains more information than \(F\).
- **Scoring sensitivity:** require the direction of the estimated high-\(C\) contrast to be reported under both domain-weighted and equal-family scores.
- **Partition sensitivity:** recompute the statistic from input-level outcomes under multiple defensible partitions that are declared before examining confirmatory scores. If conclusions depend strongly on one arbitrary partition, restrict the claim to that partition or conclude that the proposed general construct is not robust.
- **Generator and mechanism sensitivity:** report whether the signal recurs across independent mechanisms and generators, rather than letting a single generator or defect family supply all of the contrast.

The exact family controls and alternative partitions are task-specific and have not been ratified for a successor study. They must be chosen before any such study begins.

## 8. Failure criteria and interpretation rules

These are stop/interpretation rules for a future design, not a claim that any new study has been authorized.

### Gate M: mathematical/design feasibility

Mark the formulation **not identifiable in the proposed setting** if any core comparison fails because:

- no fixed-\(k\), different-\(C\) contrast exists;
- \(C\) is determined by \(k\), generator, or the declared family/carrier controls;
- independent mechanism overlap is absent; or
- the needed evaluation outcomes are not separated from candidate construction.

Do not respond to these failures by adding more replicas of the same profiles. More copies cannot create missing variation.

### Predictive support criterion

For a future confirmatory dataset, the concentration claim receives predictive support only if:

1. the prespecified overlap/identification checks pass;
2. the held-out predictive gain \(\Delta_{\mathrm{pred}}\) is positive and its prespecified 95% uncertainty interval lies above zero;
3. the fixed-\(k\) conditional association has the predicted direction (higher \(C\) predicts higher \(S\)); and
4. the direction does not reverse between the primary domain-weighted and secondary equal-family scores or collapse under the prespecified defensible partition checks.

All folds, cluster definitions, uncertainty calculations, model tuning, and multiplicity rules must be fixed before confirmatory evaluation. A positive in-sample coefficient alone cannot satisfy this criterion.

### Failure to support versus evidence against

- **Infeasible/unidentified:** the required comparisons cannot distinguish \(C\) from alternatives.
- **Inconclusive:** the estimate is too imprecise to distinguish useful gain from no gain.
- **No demonstrated predictive value:** the preregistered support criterion is not met.
- **Evidence against a meaningful effect:** an adequately precise interval excludes a prespecified minimum practically important gain, or shows the predicted direction is absent/reversed across the prespecified domain.

A minimum practically important predictive gain and an adequate sample-size/power rule have not been set in this math-only phase. They must be selected and justified before a confirmatory study can launch. The earlier \(0.10\) score-contrast planning value is not automatically a valid threshold for the new MSE-based predictive estimand.

## 9. What this model establishes now

The equations establish the accounting relationships between diagnostic profiles, concentration, evaluation failures, and weighted scores. They demonstrate numerically that:

- multiple \(C\) values are mathematically possible at \(k=5\);
- \(C\) loses information present in \(F\);
- family sizes can change the domain-weighted score when \(k\) and \(C\) are identical; and
- without a constrained diagnostic-to-evaluation relationship, diagnostic failures do not determine evaluation outcomes.

They do **not** establish that concentration predicts real solver performance, estimate the leakage relationship, validate a defect taxonomy, or make the closed parenthesis-task design viable.
