## 1. The question S1 is intended to answer

Can the candidate marginal M0/M1 prediction and parent-cluster uncertainty procedure recover a known synthetic predictive gain when the predictor is a binary diagnostic signal encoded exactly by C_D, and what does that establish about the analytical machinery?

S1 is a calibration experiment for a predictive association. It is **not** a causal experiment about changing concentration. The positive signal is generated by a latent common cause that influences both the diagnostic failure profile and held-out errors. The observed concentration is a noisy summary of the diagnostic measurement of that latent state.

This is deliberate. A useful predictor can be a proxy. S1 tests whether the analysis can measure that predictive information without putting the predictor itself into the outcome equation.

**The signal is deliberately favourable and binary.** The generator first draws a binary diagnostic signal \(Q\), and \(C_D\) is then a one-to-one recoding of \(Q\): \(Q=1\) exactly when \(C_D=0.75\), and \(Q=0\) exactly when \(C_D=0.50\). There is no information loss between \(Q\) and \(C_D\) in S1. The measurement noise is between the latent state \(H\) and \(Q\), not between \(Q\) and \(C_D\). Accordingly, S1 is a **known-positive binary-proxy recovery calibration**, not evidence that concentration is a uniquely informative scientific construct, that a maximum statistic is superior to the underlying diagnostic signal, or that real diagnostic profiles behave this way.

S1 cannot answer an empirical question about real solvers. Its sole potential decision output is methodological: whether this particular marginal estimator and parent-cluster test procedure can recover a known gain under a deliberately favourable, correctly specified binary-proxy generator. The prespecified consequences are limited:
The specific downstream decision is whether the current \(M_0/M_1\) marginal estimator plus test-parent cluster-bootstrap procedure is retained as the candidate analysis machinery for the subsequent S2-S7 validation suite:
- If every primary S1 gate is a precision-supported pass, carry this estimator/uncertainty combination forward as the **candidate** for S2-S7 specification and review. This is not an execution authorization for S2-S7 and does not validate B1.
- If any primary gate fails, do not carry the combination forward unchanged; diagnose and revise the estimator or uncertainty procedure before the S2-S7 suite is specified around it.
- If any primary gate is inconclusive, do not advance it as a pass; the next action is a separately authorized precision pilot or design revision, not automatic progression.

This is a concrete methods-selection decision: retain, revise, or stop the current candidate estimator/uncertainty combination before subsequent validation. It is not a scientific decision about whether concentration predicts real-solver correctness.

**Positive inference licensed by a complete pass:** the specified marginal six-cell estimator and test-parent cluster-bootstrap procedure can recover a known binary-proxy predictive gain under independent parent sampling on the fixed panel, within the declared operating-characteristic tolerances. This is a machinery-recovery statement only. It does not imply that \(C_D\) retains useful information from a general failure vector or that it predicts correctness in actual solvers.

Even a complete pass does not change the evidence about whether \(C_D\) predicts correctness in real solvers. S1 does not justify building the 150-variant population, making a B1 claim, or treating concentration as a generally useful summary. If the project will not use the methodological proceed/revise/stop rule above to decide whether this estimator/uncertainty combination advances to the S2-S7 validation suite, S1 has no execution purpose and should not be run.

S1 does not establish that real solvers follow this data-generating process, that the latent state is a real defect mechanism, or that concentration itself causes errors.

## 2. Estimand, target population, and scoring scale

Let:

- \(Y=1\) mean that a held-out input is answered incorrectly; \(Y=0\) means correct.
- \(X\) denote the predeclared held-out-input difficulty class.
- \(C_D\) denote the observed diagnostic concentration calculated from eight diagnostic strata.
- \(\ell(y,q)=-y\log(q)-(1-y)\log(1-q)\) denote Bernoulli log loss using natural logarithms, measured in **nats per held-out input**.

### 2.1 Target population and conditioning set

The S1 target is **conditional on the fixed, balanced 60-item held-out panel** described in §3.3. It is not a superpopulation target over newly sampled input identities. The panel contains 20 fixed items in each of three predeclared difficulty classes, and the target gives each class weight \(1/3\). Parent groups, variants, latent states, diagnostic measurements, and held-out outcomes are sampled from the generator; input identities and their difficulty labels are held fixed.

The target prediction task is a new variant from a new parent, evaluated on this fixed panel. Parent effects are unobserved at prediction time and are integrated over their declared distribution. The target therefore generalizes over new synthetic parents and variants **for this panel only**. It does not generalize to a new input panel, a new difficulty distribution, or unseen input templates.

### 2.2 Population estimand

Let \(q_0(x)=P(Y=1\mid X=x)\) and \(q_1(x,c)=P(Y=1\mid X=x,C_D=c)\) be the true marginal conditional probabilities implied by the generator, integrating over the new-parent effect and variant-level latent state. They are not fitted predictions.

For each declared signal strength \(\eta\), the population incremental log-loss gain is:

\[
\Delta^*_{\mathrm{panel}}(\eta)
=
E_\eta[\ell(Y,q_0(X))-\ell(Y,q_1(X,C_D))]
=
H_\eta(Y\mid X)-H_\eta(Y\mid X,C_D)
=
I_\eta(Y;C_D\mid X).
\]

The expectation is over the generator's parent/variant/outcome distribution and the fixed panel's equal-weighted difficulty-class distribution. It is **panel-conditional**, not input-superpopulation averaged.

Positive \(\Delta^*\) means that observing concentration improves optimal prediction of held-out error probability beyond difficulty class. Zero means no incremental information. This is a predictive-information estimand, not a causal effect.

### 2.2.1 Explicit fixed-panel definition

Let the 60 fixed input identities be indexed by \(i\), with fixed difficulty labels \(x_i\); exactly 20 have each value \(x\in\{1,2,3\}\). Write \(p_{x_i,c}(\eta)=P(Y=1\mid X=x_i,C_D=c)\) under the declared generator. The panel-conditional target at signal setting \(\eta\) is:

\[
\Delta^*_{\mathrm{panel}}(\eta)
=
\frac{1}{60}\sum_{i=1}^{60}
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{c\in\{0.50,0.75\}}
D_{\mathrm B}\!\left(p_{x_i,c}(\eta)\,\middle\|\,p_{x_i}\right).
\]

Here \(D_{\mathrm B}(a\|b)\) is the Bernoulli KL divergence defined in §4, and \(P(C_D=0.50)=P(C_D=0.75)=1/2\). Since the fixed panel contains 20 items in each difficulty class and S1 has no additional input-identity effect, this is exactly equivalent to the class-weighted expression in §4. Thus the estimand is an average over the **60 fixed panel positions**, not an expectation over new input identities.

The estimand is a function of \(\eta\). Changing \(\eta\) changes the outcome distribution \(p_{x,c}(\eta)\), and therefore changes \(\Delta^*_{\mathrm{panel}}(\eta)\); the three values in §4 are three different generator-specific panel-conditional truths. Over the declared positive grid, the gain is strictly increasing in \(\eta\): for each baseline \(p\), the derivative of the symmetric KL contribution with respect to \(d=0.6\eta\) is \(\tfrac12[\operatorname{logit}(p+d)-\operatorname{logit}(p-d)]>0\), since \(p-d\) and \(p+d\) remain in \((0,1)\).

The unit is nats of expected log-loss improvement per evaluated held-out input. This is a proper probabilistic-prediction score, not a classification-accuracy difference, a decision-utility measure, or a minimum practically important effect. No operational decision threshold is derived from it.

### 2.3 Why log loss

Bernoulli log loss is used because it is a strictly proper scoring rule for probabilistic predictions: in expectation, it is minimized by reporting the true conditional probability. Under the true conditional predictors above, the improvement has the exact information-theoretic representation \(I(Y;C_D\mid X)\), which permits an independent population-truth calculation.

The unit is nats because the logarithm is natural. Brier score, AUC, and calibration error answer different questions and do not share this exact conditional-mutual-information identity. They may be useful secondary diagnostics, but they are not interchangeable with the primary S1 estimand.

This choice is a calibration scale, not a claim that a given gain has operational decision value. The practical decision threshold is denoted \(\tau\). **S1 does not set \(\tau\)** and does not claim that its injected gain is a minimum practically important effect. The numerical \(\Delta^*\) below is a calibration truth only.

## 3. Positive-world data-generating process

### 3.1 Parent and variant hierarchy

For each independent training dataset and each independent test dataset:

1. Generate \(J\) independent parent groups, indexed by \(g\).
2. Each parent has \(V=5\) synthetic variants, indexed by \(v\).
3. Each parent receives one parent-level random effect:

\[
U_g \in \{-0.05,+0.05\},\qquad
P(U_g=-0.05)=P(U_g=+0.05)=1/2.
\]

4. Each variant receives an independent latent defect state:

\[
H_{gv}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2).
\]

The parent effect is shared by all variants and all held-out outcomes for that parent. The latent defect state is variant-specific. The \(H_{gv}\) values are independent of the parent effects.

The five-variant layout is a synthetic clustering scaffold only. It does not adopt the proposed LC518 population, mutation allocation, mechanism catalogue, or five-variants-per-parent assumption as a real-study decision.

### 3.2 Diagnostic profile and concentration

There are eight diagnostic strata. Every generated variant has exactly:

\[
k_{\mathrm{fail}}=4,\qquad
k_{\mathrm{unresolved}}=0,\qquad
B=2,
\]

where \(B\) is the number of occupied diagnostic strata.

First draw a diagnostic signal \(Q_{gv}\in\{0,1\}\):

\[
P(Q_{gv}=1\mid H_{gv}=1)=0.8,
\qquad
P(Q_{gv}=1\mid H_{gv}=0)=0.2.
\]

Then construct the eight-count failure vector \(F_{gv}=(f_1,\ldots,f_8)\):

- Choose two distinct strata uniformly from the 28 possible pairs, independently of \(H\) and \(Q\).
- If \(Q=1\), assign counts \((3,1)\) to the selected pair, choosing which member receives 3 uniformly.
- If \(Q=0\), assign counts \((2,2)\) to the selected pair.
- Set the other six counts to zero.

Thus every profile has four definite failures in exactly two strata. The concentration values are:

\[
C_D=
\begin{cases}
3/4=0.75,&Q=1,\\
2/4=0.50,&Q=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently, \(C_D\) is a **bijective encoding of \(Q\)**. S1 does not test whether a continuous or multi-valued concentration statistic can recover a noisy diagnostic signal; it tests whether the estimator can use a binary predictor that is exactly equivalent to the signal \(Q\). The maximum-count construction is deliberately arranged so that \(C_D\) carries all of \(Q\)'s information.

The identities of the occupied strata and the orientation of the counts are randomized independently of \(H\). They carry no additional information about held-out outcomes under this S1 generator.

This construction gives \(C_D\) only **two support points**, 0.50 and 0.75. S1 therefore calibrates a **binary proxy** derived from an eight-stratum count vector; it does not test estimation with a continuous or broadly multi-valued concentration distribution. The support is deliberately narrow to create a transparent first calibration contrast at fixed \(k_{\mathrm{fail}}\) and fixed breadth \(B\). That simplicity is favourable to identification and must be carried as a limitation in any interpretation.

### 3.3 Held-out input panel and known blind spot

The held-out panel has three predeclared difficulty classes \(X\in\{1,2,3\}\), with equal target weights:

\[
P(X=x)=1/3.
\]

The baseline error probabilities are:

\[
p_1=0.25,\qquad p_2=0.50,\qquad p_3=0.75.
\]

Each synthetic variant is evaluated on 60 fixed held-out inputs: 20 fixed items from each difficulty class. The same panel is used for all parents and variants. Input identity has no additional random effect in S1.

The estimand is conditional on this fixed balanced panel and its three-class target weights. **This is a known blind spot, not a neutral simplification:** S1 does not test input-level heterogeneity, shared input/template effects, input-cluster dependence, or generalization to a newly sampled held-out panel. A pass therefore gives no evidence of robustness to those features. They require a separately specified dependence scenario.

These three held-out difficulty classes are not the eight diagnostic strata. S1 does not test diagnostic-to-held-out stratum recurrence.

### 3.4 True outcome model

Let \(\eta\) be the declared positive-world signal-strength parameter. It is an analyst-selected data-generating parameter, not an estimated coefficient, a real-solver quantity, or a value derived from the fitted estimator. The pre-run signal-strength grid is \(\eta\in\{0.06,0.12,0.18\}\), with \(\eta=0.12\) the primary reference point for the calibration criteria. The smallest and largest values are sensitivity scenarios, not alternatives from which the most favourable result may be selected. For the full grid the outcome probabilities remain in the valid interval [0.02,0.98]. The implied gains are calculated in §4 rather than assumed.

For every parent \(g\), variant \(v\), and held-out input \(i\) with difficulty class \(X_i=x\), generate:

\[
Y_{gvi}\mid H_{gv},U_g,X_i=x
\sim
\operatorname{Bernoulli}
\left(
p_x+\eta(2H_{gv}-1)+U_g
\right).
\]

The Bernoulli draws are conditionally independent given \(H_{gv}\), \(U_g\), and \(X_i\). Dependence is induced because variants and held-out outcomes belonging to the same parent share \(U_g\), while all outcomes for a variant also share \(H_{gv}\).

The probability is valid throughout the pre-run signal grid. At the largest value \(\eta=0.18\), its minimum is \(0.25-0.18-0.05=0.02\) and its maximum is \(0.75+0.18+0.05=0.98\).

**Crucially, \(C_D\) does not appear in this outcome equation.** The outcome is generated from the latent state \(H\), parent effect \(U\), and input difficulty \(X\). The diagnostic profile is generated separately from the same latent state \(H\).

The intended dependency is:

\[
H\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow C_D,
\qquad
H\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
U_g\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
X\longrightarrow Y.
\]

There is no arrow \(C_D\longrightarrow Y\). Concentration is a noisy predictive proxy for \(H\), not a causal treatment.

The selected signal-strength values are synthetic design parameters only. Their range is included to reveal how calibration changes with the size of the known predictive signal; it has no empirical grounding in real solver behaviour.

## 4. Independent derivation of the true predictive gain

The derivation uses only the generator in §3. It does not use fitted regression coefficients, training outcomes, test outcomes, or the estimator's observed score.

Since \(P(H=1)=1/2\), \(P(Q=1\mid H=1)=0.8\), and \(P(Q=1\mid H=0)=0.2\), Bayes' rule gives:

\[
P(Q=1)=1/2,\qquad
P(H=1\mid Q=1)=0.8,\qquad
P(H=1\mid Q=0)=0.2.
\]

For a new parent, \(U_g\) is independent of \(H,Q,X\) and has equal probability at \(-0.05\) and \(+0.05\). Thus the required marginalization is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(Y=1\mid X=x,Q=q)
&=\sum_{h=0}^{1}\sum_{u\in\{-0.05,+0.05\}}
\left[p_x+\eta(2h-1)+u\right]
P(H=h\mid Q=q)P(U=u)\\
&=p_x+\eta\left[2P(H=1\mid Q=q)-1\right]
+\tfrac12(-0.05+0.05).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(C_D=0.75\) if and only if \(Q=1\), and \(C_D=0.50\) if and only if \(Q=0\), the conditional probability shifts are \(+0.6\eta\) and \(-0.6\eta\). At the primary reference point \(\eta=0.12\), these are \(+0.072\) and \(-0.072\):

\[
P(Y=1\mid X=x,C_D=0.75)=p_x+0.072,
\]

\[
P(Y=1\mid X=x,C_D=0.50)=p_x-0.072.
\]

The parent effect integrates to zero in these **single-variant marginal probabilities** because \(U_g\) is independent of \(C_D\) and has mean zero. This does not remove the shared-parent dependence across variants: it remains in the joint distribution and is preserved by parent-cluster resampling in §5.5.

Without concentration:

\[
P(Y=1\mid X=x)=p_x.
\]

The two concentration values each occur with probability one half. Define the Bernoulli Kullback-Leibler divergence:

\[
D_{\mathrm B}(a\|b)
=
a\log\frac{a}{b}
+(1-a)\log\frac{1-a}{1-b}.
\]

For any value of \(\eta\) in the pre-run grid, define \(d_\eta=0.6\eta\). The exact panel-conditional population gain is:

\[
\Delta^*(\eta)
=
\frac{1}{3}
\sum_{p\in\{0.25,0.50,0.75\}}
\frac{1}{2}
\left[
D_{\mathrm B}(p+d_\eta\|p)
+
D_{\mathrm B}(p-d_\eta\|p)
\right].
\]
Because the panel contains exactly 20 inputs at each of the three values of \(X\), this equal-class expression is exactly the fixed-panel estimand \(\Delta^*_{\mathrm{panel}}(\eta)\) defined in §2.2.1.

The per-class arithmetic for every predeclared \(\eta\) is:

| \(\eta\) | \(d_\eta\) | \(p=0.25\) contribution | \(p=0.50\) contribution | \(p=0.75\) contribution | \(\Delta^*_{\mathrm{panel}}(\eta)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.06 | 0.036 | 0.003465365171158107 | 0.002594244144740597 | 0.003465365171158127 | 0.003174991495685611 |
| 0.12 | 0.072 | 0.013977633425200666 | 0.010404132356061697 | 0.013977633425200597 | 0.012786466402154320 |
| 0.18 | 0.108 | 0.031916995456899450 | 0.023512871011099112 | 0.031916995456899450 | 0.029115620641632672 |

Each class contribution is the average of the two KL terms for that class; the final column is their equal-weighted mean. In particular:

\[
\boxed{\Delta^*_{\mathrm{panel}}(0.12)=0.01278646640215432\ \text{nats per held-out input}.}

\]

The values are directly reproducible from the displayed Bernoulli-KL formula. They are not empirical results, not a practical threshold \(\tau\), and not claims about real solvers. The grid is intended to expose sensitivity to signal strength; no result may be selected post hoc as the preferred S1 result.

### 4.1 What the full-vector equality does and does not establish

Under this particular S1 construction, the full failure vector contains no information about held-out outcomes beyond \(C_D\). To show this, write the vector \(F\) equivalently as \((Q,A)\), where \(Q\) determines whether the selected pair receives counts \((3,1)\) or \((2,2)\), and \(A\) records the randomly selected pair and, when relevant, the random orientation of the count 3.

By construction, \(A\) is generated independently of \(H,U,X,Y\) conditional on \(Q\). Therefore:

\[
I(Y;F\mid X)
=I(Y;Q,A\mid X)
=I(Y;Q\mid X)+I(Y;A\mid Q,X)
=I(Y;Q\mid X).
\]

Since \(C_D\) is a one-to-one function of \(Q\) in this design:

\[
\boxed{I(Y;F\mid X)=I(Y;C_D\mid X).}
\]

This equality is proved for S1's deliberately constructed measurement process. **It is a favourable feature, not evidence that concentration generally preserves the information in the full failure vector.** S1 has made every non-\(C_D\) detail of \(F\) ancillary by construction. A later scenario must allow the full vector to carry predictive information not captured by its maximum, and must assess the loss from that compression.

## 5. Candidate estimator and predictive evaluation

This section specifies a deliberately transparent marginal estimator for review. It does not claim to reproduce the full B1 v1.6 conditional-randomization procedure. Whether S1 must instead instantiate that exact B1 estimator is an explicit reviewer question in §10.

### 5.1 Independent training and test parents

Each simulation replication generates two independent parent samples from the same generator:

- a training sample of \(J\) parents;
- a test sample of \(J\) different parents.

The values are \(J\in\{30,60,120\}\). They are calibration-grid points only. They do not select or recommend an operating parent count for LC518. Each replication therefore uses \(2J\) independent parents across its training and test samples.

The same five variants per parent and 60-input panel are used in both samples.

### 5.2 Two prespecified prediction models

Both models predict held-out error probability and are fitted only on the training-parent sample.

**Baseline \(M_0\):**

\[
\operatorname{logit}P(Y=1\mid X=x)=\alpha_x.
\]

There is one intercept for each of the three difficulty classes.

**Augmented \(M_1\):**

\[
\operatorname{logit}P(Y=1\mid X=x,C_D=c)=\alpha_{x,c}.
\]

There is one intercept for each of the six difficulty-by-concentration cells. This is a saturated categorical representation of the observable conditional probability table. It contains no latent \(H\), no parent identifier, and no direct outcome-generating term for \(C_D\).

Fit by ordinary binomial maximum likelihood. This is a working likelihood for the prediction functions: the point estimates are obtained from the row-level Bernoulli likelihood, while no row-independent coefficient standard errors or p-values are used for inference. Dependence is retained in the generated parent clusters, assessed across independent training replications, and handled in the independent test uncertainty procedure. No post-fit feature selection, tuning, or threshold selection is permitted. A non-convergent or non-estimable fit makes that replication unavailable; no substitute estimator is silently introduced.

Predictions for test parents are population-marginal predictions. The parent effect is not known for a new parent; its distribution is integrated into the target conditional probabilities. Parent clustering is handled in the uncertainty procedure below.

**Deliberately favourable calibration feature:** because \(C_D\) has only two values and \(X\) has three values, \(M_1\) is saturated over the six observable \((X,C_D)\) cells. Its functional form can represent the generator's true marginal conditional probabilities exactly. S1 therefore tests finite-sample learning and uncertainty for a correctly specified binary-proxy estimator. It does **not** test robustness to a misspecified estimator, a continuous or multi-valued concentration feature, or a more complex relationship. Those are not silently covered by a pass.

### 5.3 Formal known-truth target for each fitted training pair

The Bayes population gain \(\Delta^*\) in §4 is the fixed limit available to the optimal predictors. It is **not** the target of the conditional test-parent interval.

For each fitted training pair, define its replication-specific known truth \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit,panel}}\) as the exact expected log-loss difference of those two fitted prediction functions under the same fixed-panel generator distribution:

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{fit,panel}}
=
\frac{1}{60}\sum_{i=1}^{60}\frac{1}{2}
\sum_{c\in\{0.50,0.75\}}
\left[
h(p_{x_i,c}(\eta),\widehat q_0(x_i))
-
h(p_{x_i,c}(\eta),\widehat q_1(x_i,c))
\right].
\]

The cross-entropy function and the generator-specific conditional probabilities are defined as follows:

\[
h(p,q)=-p\log q-(1-p)\log(1-q),
\]

\[
p_{x,0.75}(\eta)=p_x+0.6\eta,\qquad
p_{x,0.50}(\eta)=p_x-0.6\eta,
\]

and \(P(X=x)=1/3\), \(P(C_D=c)=1/2\).

This is the known population expected log-loss difference for this particular fitted pair. It is computed analytically from the generator after fitting, without using any test outcomes. It is a **known-truth benchmark conditional on the training fit**, not an estimate of \(\Delta^*\). It may be smaller than \(\Delta^*\), zero, or negative if finite-sample fitting fails to learn the signal.

The two calibration questions are distinct:

- Does the fitted learning procedure approach the Bayes limit? Assess the distribution of \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\) across independent training-parent samples against \(\Delta^*\).
- Does the independent test estimator recover the gain of the fitted pair? Assess \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{test}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\) and the interval coverage conditional on that fitted pair.

The bias and coverage criteria in §7 refer to the second question. They do not claim that the conditional test interval covers \(\Delta^*\) or accounts for training-sample variability.

### 5.4 Independent test estimate

On the independent test-parent sample, calculate paired held-out log losses from \(M_0\) and \(M_1\).

For each test parent \(g\), first average the loss difference over that parent's variants and held-out inputs:

\[
d_g=
\frac{1}{VI}
\sum_{v=1}^{V}\sum_{i=1}^{I}
\left[
\ell(Y_{gvi},\widehat q_0(X_i))
-
\ell(Y_{gvi},\widehat q_1(X_i,C_{D,gv}))
\right].
\]

Then give each parent equal weight:

\[
\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{test}}
=
\frac{1}{J}\sum_{g=1}^{J}d_g.
\]

This is an estimate of the fitted pair's conditional true gain \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\), not a new estimate of \(\Delta^*\) from the training data.

### 5.5 Uncertainty interval and exact scope

Use a **nonparametric parent-cluster bootstrap of the independent test sample**, conditional on the already fitted training prediction functions:

1. Resample the \(J\) complete test-parent clusters with replacement.
2. Keep each selected parent's five variants and all 60 outcomes per variant together.
3. Recalculate the equal-parent-weighted mean loss difference.
4. Repeat for 1,999 bootstrap draws.
5. Use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles for the two-sided 95% interval.
6. For the one-sided 95% lower bound used in the positive-detection and false-positive criteria, use the 5th percentile of the same bootstrap distribution, not the 2.5th percentile.

The bootstrap distribution approximates the sampling distribution of \(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{test}}\) over new independent parent clusters, **conditional on the fitted training models and the fixed held-out panel**. Resampled clusters are drawn with replacement; the resulting bootstrap dependence is the standard nonparametric cluster-bootstrap construction. No parametric correction is applied.

The bootstrap does not capture training-sample variability. That is deliberate because this interval targets the replication-specific \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\). Training-sample variability is separately assessed across the independent simulation replications through the distribution of \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\).

For an independently generated training sample \(T\), let \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}(T)\) be its fitted-pair truth and let \(CI(T,S)\) be the interval from an independent test-parent sample \(S\). The conditional coverage for that fitted training pair is:

\[
\gamma(T)=P_S\{\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}(T)\in CI(T,S)\mid T\}.
\]

The operating-characteristic coverage averaged over independently sampled training fits is:

\[
\Gamma=E_T[\gamma(T)].
\]

The reported coverage proportion across simulation replications estimates \(\Gamma\): it averages test-sample coverage conditional on each replication's training fit over the training-sample distribution. It is **not** coverage of \(\Delta^*\), and it is **not** a single interval that incorporates both training- and test-sample uncertainty. The bootstrap within each replication remains conditional on that replication's fitted functions and resamples only complete test-parent clusters.

No row-level or variant-level bootstrap is allowed. This procedure does not generalize to new input panels or templates; those require a later crossed-input scenario.

## 6. Fully specified matched null companion for false-positive calibration

A positive scenario alone cannot assess false-positive behaviour. The matched null changes only the outcome relationship between latent state \(H\) and held-out errors. All other S1 components remain identical.

**Unchanged from S1 positive world:**

- \(J\) independent training parents and \(J\) independent test parents at each calibration-grid point;
- five variants per parent;
- parent effect \(U_g\in\{-0.05,+0.05\}\) with equal probability, shared across all variants and held-out outcomes for that parent;
- variant-level \(H_{gv}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\);
- diagnostic signal \(Q\) with sensitivity 0.8 and false-positive probability 0.2;
- the eight-stratum construction of \(F\), so \(k_{\mathrm{fail}}=4\), \(k_{\mathrm{unresolved}}=0\), two occupied strata, and \(C_D\in\{0.50,0.75\}\);
- the same fixed 60-item held-out panel, with 20 items in each difficulty class and the same baseline values \(p_1=0.25,p_2=0.50,p_3=0.75\);
- the same M0 and M1 estimators, train/test separation, parent-cluster bootstrap, and availability rules.

**The only changed parameter is `eta=0`** in the outcome model:

\[
Y_{gvi}\mid H_{gv},U_g,X_i=x
\sim
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_x+U_g).
\]

The latent state still predicts \(Q\), \(F\), and \(C_D\), but it no longer affects held-out outcomes. The parent effect is independent of \(H,Q,C_D,X\), and its marginal mean is zero. Therefore:

\[
P(Y=1\mid X=x,C_D=c)=p_x,
\qquad
Y\perp C_D\mid X,
\qquad
\boxed{\Delta^*=0.}
\]

The shared \(U_g\) still induces within-parent outcome dependence. This is important: the null preserves the same parent clustering as the positive world. A false-positive difference cannot be attributed merely to removal of the parent random effect.

This matched null is the minimum false-positive companion for S1. It is matched on unresolvedness only in the narrow sense that both positive and null worlds have k_unresolved=0. No unresolved outcome is generated in either world, so this does not test unresolved-outcome classification, exclusion, informative missingness, or any handling rule when unresolved outcomes occur. It is not the complete S2–S7 validation programme and does not establish false-positive control under other forms of confounding, input dependence, partition changes, or unresolvedness. S6 must introduce unresolved outcomes and specify their relationship to held-out errors and the analysis rule.

## 7. Simulation dimensions and proposed calibration tolerances

These are **proposals for review, not frozen criteria**. Their rationales and limitations are stated so that the reviewer can accept, reject, or replace them before any run.

### 7.1 Dimensions

| Quantity | Proposed value |
|---|---:|
| Independent training parents, \(J\) | 30, 60, 120 |
| Independent test parents, \(J\) | 30, 60, 120 |
| Variants per parent, \(V\) | 5 |
| Held-out inputs per variant, \(I\) | 60 |
| Inputs per difficulty class | 20 |
| Independent positive-world replications per \(J\) | 2,000 |
| Independent matched-null replications per \(J\) | 2,000 |
| Test-parent bootstrap draws per dataset | 1,999 |
| Primary score | Bernoulli log loss, nats per held-out input |

The parent grid doubles from 30 to 60 to 120, spanning a fourfold range in independent training and test parent counts. If the parent-level variance of the paired test loss difference were constant, its standard error would scale approximately as \(1/\sqrt J\), giving relative standard-error factors \(1,1/\sqrt2,1/2\). The absolute interval widths cannot be responsibly supplied without the parent-level variance under the fitted procedure; that variance is not estimated in the record and is a target of the calibration. This grid is therefore a declared diagnostic range, not a claim that any point is adequate or that it spans a particular expected interval width.

The \(J\) values are calibration-grid dimensions only. They are not an operating sample-size recommendation, do not adopt the LC518 30-parent roster, and do not answer the real-study parent-count question. Training and test parents are separate, so each replication uses \(2J\) independent parents in total.

### 7.2 Primary operating point, reporting grid, and proposed pass criteria

The positive-world grid has three values of \(J\) and three values of \(\eta\). The decision role of every positive-world cell is explicit below.

| Signal \(\eta\) | \(J=30\) | \(J=60\) | \(J=120\) |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.06 | Report-only diagnostic | Report-only diagnostic | Report-only sensitivity |
| 0.12 | Report-only diagnostic | Report-only diagnostic | **Primary calibration gate** |
| 0.18 | Report-only diagnostic | Report-only diagnostic | Report-only sensitivity |

Only \(J=120,\eta=0.12\) is the primary positive-world pass point. No result at another grid point may substitute for it. All nine cells must still be reported. The smaller-\(J\) cells characterize the cluster-count trajectory; the other \(\eta\) values characterize signal-strength sensitivity. They are not separate pass gates in this first-stage calibration.

At the primary point, the proposed criteria are:

1. **Learning recovery:** define the replication-level recovery ratio \(r_j=\Delta_{\mathrm{fit},j}/\Delta^*(0.12)\). Its mean across independent training replications must be at least 0.80. Since \(\Delta^*(0.12)\) is fixed across replications at the primary point, this is equivalent to \(E[\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}]\geq0.8\Delta^*(0.12)\). The 80% threshold is a **provisional engineering calibration choice**, not a statistical necessity, a decision-theoretic threshold, or a practical-effect threshold. Report sensitivity verdicts at 70% and 90%.
2. **Test-estimator bias:** define \(b_r=\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{test},r}-\Delta_{\mathrm{fit},r}\). The absolute Monte Carlo mean of \(b_r\) must be no more than 10% of \(\Delta^*(0.12)\), or 0.001278646640215432 nats per input. This assesses recovery of the fitted-pair truth, not the Bayes limit. The 10% value is a **provisional engineering tolerance**, not a decision-theoretic or empirically established precision threshold. Report sensitivity at 5% and 20%.
3. **Interval coverage:** the parent-cluster two-sided 95% intervals cover the replication-specific \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\) in 94% to 96% of replications. The coverage estimand is \(\Gamma\) in §5.5, namely average test-parent coverage conditional on each training fit, averaged over training fits. This gate applies only at \(J=120,\eta=0.12\). Coverage at other positive-world grid points is reported but not a pass gate.
4. **Positive detection:** at least 80% of the primary-point one-sided 95% parent-cluster lower bounds exceed zero. This criterion is evaluated **only at \(J=120,\eta=0.12\)**. Detection at other \(J\) or \(\eta\) is reported as a sensitivity result, not used to choose the primary result.
5. **Availability:** failed fits, empty bootstrap draws, and unavailable replications are counted and reported at every grid point. No failed replication is replaced. If more than 1% of attempted replications are unavailable at the primary point, that positive-world cell is not passed. The same 1% limit applies to each matched-null \(J\) cell. Report sensitivity at 0.5% and 2%.

The matched null has \(\eta=0\), with the same diagnostic signal, parent hierarchy, panel, estimator and bootstrap. Its false-positive gate is separate from the positive-world matrix: the proportion of one-sided 95% lower bounds above zero must not exceed 6% **at each** \(J\in\{30,60,120\}\). The null gate therefore tests all three parent counts, not only the primary point. For each \(J\), let \(K_J\) be the false-positive count and \(R_{\mathrm{valid},J}\) the number of valid null replications:

\[
\widehat{\mathrm{FPR}}_J=\frac{K_J}{R_{\mathrm{valid},J}},
\qquad
MCSE(\widehat{\mathrm{FPR}}_J)
=
\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\mathrm{FPR}}_J(1-\widehat{\mathrm{FPR}}_J)}
{R_{\mathrm{valid},J}}}.
\]

If all 2,000 attempted null replications are valid (so \(R_{\mathrm{valid},J}=2{,}000\)), the reference MCSE is approximately 0.004873 at a 5% false-positive rate and 0.005310 at 6%. These same calculations apply separately to \(J=30,60,120\); the actual denominator must be reported for each:

| Null parent count \(J\) | Planned attempted replications | Reference MCSE at 5% FPR | Reference MCSE at 6% FPR |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 | 2,000 | 0.004873 | 0.005310 |
| 60 | 2,000 | 0.004873 | 0.005310 |
| 120 | 2,000 | 0.004873 | 0.005310 |

The values are identical across \(J\) only because the planned replication count is identical. The 2,000 values are attempted replications, not a promise of 2,000 valid cases; any unavailable replications change \(R_{\mathrm{valid},J}\) and therefore the MCSE. Report the observed rate and its exact binomial Monte Carlo interval; a point estimate at or below 6% is not proof that the population false-positive probability is at most 6%.

The relative bias rule has the following absolute equivalents. These values are shown for all \(\eta\) so that the scale is visible; only the \(J=120,\eta=0.12\) cell is the primary bias gate.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

All thresholds remain **provisional engineering tolerances**, not statistical necessities or decision-theoretic cut-offs. The 80% recovery and 10% bias values have no empirical variance basis before calibration; their achieved Monte Carlo precision is therefore unknown until the permitted replication-level variances are measured. They may be reconsidered after a separately authorized pilot, but any revised tolerance must be documented and frozen before a distinct confirmatory calibration run. Pilot replications must not be pooled with or counted toward the confirmatory 2,000 replications. The 6% false-positive ceiling is approximately two Monte Carlo standard errors above a nominal 5% rate at 2,000 replications. The 94%-96% coverage band is a tolerance around nominal 95% coverage. The 80% detection target is a design-power target at the one declared positive reference point. None of these quantities defines the operational value \(\tau\).

### 7.3 Monte Carlo precision and reporting

There are \(R=2{,}000\) independent simulation replications per positive \((J,\eta)\) cell and per matched-null \(J\) cell. The \(1{,}999\) resamples inside a dataset's parent-cluster bootstrap are not Monte Carlo replications and must not be counted as such.

For an estimated operating-characteristic proportion \(\widehat r\), including coverage, positive detection, false-positive rate, and availability, report:

\[
MCSE(\widehat r)=\sqrt{\frac{\widehat r(1-\widehat r)}{R_{\mathrm{valid}}}},
\]

where `R_rate` is the denominator for the particular rate: `R_valid` for coverage, detection, and false-positive rates among valid replications, and `R_attempted = 2,000` for the availability rate. With 2,000 contributing replications, illustrative standard errors are 0.004873 at a rate of 0.95 or 0.05, 0.008944 at 0.80, and 0.005310 at 0.06. Report an exact binomial Monte Carlo interval alongside each rate.

For the learning-recovery gate, calculate \(\bar r=R_{\mathrm{valid}}^{-1}\sum_j r_j\) and \(MCSE(\bar r)=s_r/\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{valid}}}\). For the bias gate, calculate \(\bar b=R_{\mathrm{valid}}^{-1}\sum_j b_j\) and \(MCSE(\bar b)=s_b/\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{valid}}}\). Here \(s_r\) and \(s_b\) are the sample standard deviations of the replication-level recovery ratios and biases, respectively. Their 95% Monte Carlo intervals are:

\[
\bar r \pm t_{0.975,R_{\mathrm{valid}}-1}\frac{s_r}{\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{valid}}}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bar b \pm t_{0.975,R_{\mathrm{valid}}-1}\frac{s_b}{\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{valid}}}}.
\]

These formulas are the prespecified numerical precision rules; the numerical \(s_r\) and \(s_b\) cannot be known until the independently generated simulation replications exist.

For both gates, an interval that overlaps the pass region and the fail region is **inconclusive**, not a pass. For transparent gate adjudication, call learning recovery a precision-supported pass only if the 95% Monte Carlo interval for \(E[r]\) lies entirely at or above 0.80; call it a fail if the interval lies entirely below 0.80; otherwise call it **inconclusive**. Call the bias gate a precision-supported pass only if the 95% Monte Carlo interval for \(E[b]\) lies entirely inside the equivalence band \([-0.1\Delta^*(0.12),+0.1\Delta^*(0.12)]\); call it a fail if the interval lies entirely outside that band on either side; otherwise call it **inconclusive**. These conservative Monte Carlo decision rules are stated separately from the point-estimate tolerances.

For any other replication-level scalar \(Z_r\), including squared error or interval width, report \(MCSE(\overline Z)=s_Z/\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{valid}}}\) and the corresponding 95% Monte Carlo interval. A mean or rate that is numerically inside its tolerance but too imprecise to distinguish values outside the tolerance must be labelled **inconclusive**, not a clean pass.

All nine positive-world cells must report mean and median \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\), mean and median test estimate, bias, RMSE, interval width, coverage, positive-detection fraction, and availability reasons. All three matched-null cells must report false-positive fraction and availability. Report the MCSE and Monte Carlo interval for each operating-characteristic estimate; do not choose a more favourable \(\eta\), \(J\), or sensitivity tolerance after seeing results.

## 8. Circularity assessment

The intended circularity objection is: the generator must not create outcomes from the same predictor equation the estimator is then asked to rediscover.

S1 avoids that direct circularity in the following way:

1. The outcome generator is written first and uses latent \(H\), parent effect \(U\), and difficulty \(X\). It contains no \(C_D\), no fitted coefficient for \(C_D\), and no term selected to match the estimator.
2. Diagnostic failures are generated from \(H\) through a separate noisy measurement channel.
3. \(C_D\) is calculated only after the diagnostic failure vector is generated.
4. The population conditional probabilities \(P(Y\mid X,C_D)\) and \(\Delta^*\) are derived by marginalizing the declared joint distribution of \(H,U,Q\). They are not taken from a fitted model.
5. The finite-training fitted gain \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fit}}\) is calculated from the generator's known conditional outcome distribution, independently of the test outcomes. The independent test estimator is then checked against it.

The six-cell logistic estimator can represent the observable conditional probability table after marginalizing the latent state. That representational compatibility is not itself circular: the generator never uses the observed concentration value to draw \(Y\). The relationship arises because \(C_D\) is informative about a common latent cause.

This compatibility is nevertheless a **favourable feature**. The true conditional model has exactly six \((X,C_D)\) cells, and \(M_1\) is saturated over those same six cells. S1 therefore evaluates the finite-sample performance of a correctly specified estimator. It does not test functional-form robustness or recovery under estimator misspecification. A separate misspecification arm would be required before making any such claim.

**What this does not test:** it does not distinguish a useful proxy from a causal effect of concentration. S1 is a positive calibration world for incremental prediction. The later confounded scenario must make the relevant latent state observed and included among the baseline controls; its conditional incremental gain for \(C_D\) should then be zero.

If the project requires a positive control for a direct causal effect of concentration rather than a predictive proxy, this S1 does not meet that requirement and must be rejected or renamed. Its positive world should be described as a **known-positive binary-proxy world**, not as a demonstration that concentration itself is a causal or uniquely informative construct.

The S3 proposal should be split into two stages:
- **S3a, oracle adjustment:** preserve the unadjusted proxy association, make the true common cause \(H\) observable, and compare a baseline conditioned on \(X,H\) against an augmented model conditioned on \(X,H,C_D\). Under this generator, \(Y\perp C_D\mid(X,H)\), so the known conditional incremental gain from adding \(C_D\) is zero. This checks the best-case result when the common cause is known exactly.
- **S3b, imperfect-proxy adjustment:** replace full observation of \(H\) with a declared noisy proxy or partial measurement \(W\) of \(H\), with a prespecified sensitivity/specificity or measurement model. Compare \(X,W\) against \(X,W,C_D\), and derive the corresponding known conditional gain independently. This tests whether \(C_D\) adds information beyond a feasible but imperfect common-cause measurement, rather than only beyond an oracle.

S3a is a useful diagnostic first step but is not sufficient for a real-world adjustment claim. S3b is required before the project can assess the imperfect-measurement problem relevant to a non-oracle analysis. Even S3a and S3b remain synthetic scenarios and cannot by themselves establish real-solver validity. A scenario that merely sets \(\eta=0\) is the matched null in §6; it is not a substitute for either S3 stage. S3a and S3b remain proposals and are not specified or frozen by S1.

## 9. Scope limits and exact inference-transfer statement

S1 deliberately holds the following fixed or absent:

- one homogeneous synthetic parent population;
- one latent binary defect state, not eight causal defect mechanisms;
- exactly four definite failures and two occupied diagnostic strata;
- no unresolved diagnostic outcomes;
- a binary concentration proxy with only \(C_D=0.50\) and \(C_D=0.75\);
- a fixed held-out input panel with three difficulty classes;
- no random input-group or template effect;
- no diagnostic-to-held-out stratum correspondence;
- no mutation-operator identity or realization-setting variable;
- a correctly specified saturated six-cell estimator.

Consequently, S1 cannot validate measurement robustness, the proposed stratum-recurrence mechanism, identification against observed defect identity, unresolved-outcome handling, generalization to new input panels, multi-valued concentration, or robustness to estimator misspecification.

> **Inference-transfer boundary.** Passing S1 licenses only the claim that the specified marginal saturated six-cell estimator can recover the known panel-conditional predictive gain of a binary diagnostic signal when that signal is encoded exactly by \(C_D\), under this particular independent-parent, fixed-input-panel generator. The signal \(Q\) is measured with error relative to latent \(H\), but the mapping \(Q\leftrightarrow C_D\) is error-free and one-to-one. A pass is therefore entirely compatible with \(C_D\) being useless as a **general summary statistic** of real or arbitrary failure vectors. It does not show that the maximum of a general failure vector preserves its information, that concentration is a uniquely informative scientific construct, or that the generator represents real solvers.

**What a pass does not license:** it does not validate B1 v1.6, its conditional-randomization test, its exact-cell selection, its parent-population inference, its multi-valued or eight-stratum concentration support, or any claim about real solvers. It does not authorize LC518 solver construction or execution.

S1 uses a deliberately marginal estimator as a first calibration step. The exact B1 v1.6 procedure is a different analysis target. Before anyone can claim that the B1 analysis is calibrated, a separate bridge calibration must either:

1. apply the exact B1 v1.6 estimator and its declared conditional-randomization procedure to a generator with a known truth for that same estimand and target; or
2. formally revise the intended B1 analysis to the estimator calibrated here, through the appropriate review and acceptance process.

A pass in this S1 alone cannot be transferred to B1 v1.6.

The remaining validation scenarios are:

- S2: the matched null in §6, extended with complete false-positive controls;
- S3a: an oracle common-cause control, conditioning on fully observed \(H\).
- S3b: an imperfect-proxy/partial-measurement control, with a prespecified noisy measurement \(W\) of \(H\), comparing \((X,W)\) with \((X,W,C_D)\). Neither stage is a real-solver validation;
- S4: predeclared alternative diagnostic partitions, with the same underlying input-level failure outcomes reclassified;
- S5: crossed parent, solver, input-group, and template dependence;
- S6: a required unresolved-outcome scenario. It is **named but not specified or frozen** here. Its future specification must declare the unresolved-outcome process, its association with held-out correctness, the causal/dependence DAG, how unresolved outcomes enter the \((k_{\mathrm{fail}},k_{\mathrm{unresolved}})\) coordinates, the estimator's treatment rule, and the corresponding known-truth and null checks;
- S7: degenerate and unsupported cells, with explicit unavailability rather than pooling or substitution.

No S2-S7 scenario is frozen by this S1 document. S3a/S3b and S6 are named requirements only; they are not complete executable scenario specifications. In particular, S6 is not being treated as already designed merely because it appears in this list.
