**Revision log v1.4 → v1.5 (GPT adjudication of v1.4: HOLD, 2026-11-14; source GPT, with Claude's independent v1.4 content review; each disposition verified against the v1.4 text by GLM).** GPT's ruling, recorded as binding for this revision: the Monte Carlo availability condition is **symmetric** — the same deterministic fitting and availability rule is applied to the observed assignment and to every sampled assignment — so the Monte Carlo plus-one rank test remains super-uniform **conditional on the availability event** \(A=\{F(B_0)=F(B_1)=\cdots=F(B_{R_{\mathrm{perm}}})=1\}\), where \(F(b)=1\) denotes that the complete fitting procedure succeeds for assignment \(b\), with the comparison-set members uniform over the fit-available subset \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}^{\mathrm{fit}}=\{b\in\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}:F(b)=1\}\). Claude's option 1 (restricting the confirmatory test to the exact-enumeration branch) was **not adopted**, and no numerical substitute value for failed fits was introduced. The blocking defect GPT identified in v1.4 was not a bias in the Monte Carlo branch but an **implementation contradiction**: §5.7 V5(b) required successful fitting for **every admissible assignment's** pipeline — implementable under exact enumeration, which evaluates all of \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\), but **not** under Monte Carlo, which evaluates only the observed and sampled assignments and cannot establish that unsampled admissible assignments would fit. This revision makes the narrow availability-semantics corrections below and nothing more: **no new resampling procedure, no redesign, and no change to any settled section**. GPT also accepted Claude's other v1.4 observations — in particular that Firth-trigger variation across assignments does not invalidate the test, because the trigger and refit rule are deterministic functions of each assignment and the complete pipeline therefore defines \(T(b)\) — with the corresponding reporting duty retained.

## 0. Role of this plan

This plan operationalises the six items Rev 1.4 §6.1 requires it to specify **before any fitting or inspection of held-out outcomes**: (i) the confirmatory test for \(\Delta LL\) under overlapping LOPO folds, with the resampling unit; (ii) treatment of unequal scored-set coverage across parents; (iii) the role of labelled sensitivities; (iv) fold definitions, regularization, and convergence criteria; (v) multiplicity handling across co-reported secondary summaries; and (vi) the N4-a operationalisation per Amendment E. Sections 1–4 fix data, estimand, folds, and fitting; §5 fixes the confirmatory test, with its validity conditions and honest failure modes stated in §5.7; §6 fixes statuses; §7 fixes multiplicity; §8 operationalises N4-a; §§9–10 fix the N2 regime-audit integration and the N3 reading with its proxy-pathway check; §11 consolidates reporting; §12 states boundaries; §13 maps compliance.

---

## 1. Data, units, eligibility

1. **Analysis rows.** The unit of the primary likelihood is the version × canonical-record row: \(Y_{v,r} \in \{0,1\}\) for mutant version \(v\) on development record \(r \in \{1,\dots,78\}\) (Rev 1.4 §6.1). The same fixed 78-record bank is used across versions; it is **development data, publicly exposed, never sealed**. Diagnostic tables from the oracle supply each version's identity, family, totals, and \(C_D(v) = M(v)/k(v)\). The frozen blind E3 is untouched by this plan.
2. **Primary sample.** Versions \(v\) whose full-population cell \(c(v) = (q, Z, k, \ell)\) satisfies \(B1(c) = 1\): \(k \ge 2\), \(G_c \ge 3\) distinct parents, \(D_c \ge 2\) distinct observed \(C_D\) values (Rev 1.4 §4.1, §6.1). No pooling across identities, families, or exact totals. Versions outside qualifying cells are not added to the primary estimand; any broader scoring is a separately labelled secondary analysis (Rev 1.4 §6.1).
3. **Eligibility is recomputed per dataset, by the design-only audit.** Cell support \(B1(c)\), fold-level estimability, and scored sets are recomputed by the **design-only audit** (§3 item 3 conditions; §5.3 step 0) from each dataset's own diagnostic tables and design — the real dataset, or each §8 synthetic replication. **Outcomes never determine eligibility, scored sets, or admissibility** (§5.7 V2). Within a dataset, the audit's selection decisions — the declared audit-equality key (§5.3 step 1) — are fixed across all admissible assignments; eligibility is never carried over from another dataset.

## 2. Predeclared estimand

The primary statistic is the equal-parent \(\Delta LL\) of Rev 1.4 §6.1, restated for self-containment:

\[
\Delta LL=\frac{1}{|\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}}(L_{0,g}-L_{1,g}),\qquad
L_{m,g}=(|\mathcal V_g^{\mathrm{score}}|\,|\mathcal R|)^{-1}\!\!\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_g^{\mathrm{score}}}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\bigl[-Y_{v,r}\log\widehat\pi^{(m)}_{v,r}-(1-Y_{v,r})\log(1-\widehat\pi^{(m)}_{v,r})\bigr],
\]

with \(\mathcal R = \{1,\dots,78\}\), \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}=\{g:|\mathcal V_g^{\mathrm{score}}|>0\}\), \(m=0\) the exact-cell baseline and \(m=1\) the enriched model; positive values favour the enriched model. The population quantity targeted is the expected per-record log-likelihood gain of the enriched model over the exact-cell baseline, under the LOPO prediction rule declared in §3, averaged with **equal weight per scored parent**. Parents with no eligible scored rows are outside the estimand; their exclusion reasons are reported.

## 3. Folds, scored sets, and unequal coverage (Rev 1.4 item ii; fold definitions)

1. **Folds.** Exactly leave-one-parent-out (LOPO) over the parents of the primary sample. Fold \(g\): training rows = all rows of primary-sample versions whose parent \(\ne g\); test rows = parent \(g\)'s primary-sample rows. No fold variants, no repeats, no data-driven fold construction. Controls \(S0\) and versions with \(k = 0\) are outside the primary sample (\(C_D\) undefined; Rev 1.4 §7.2 \(k=0\) convention).
2. **Scored set.** For each fold, both models are scored on exactly the same test rows: the parent's versions in cells that remain present in the training fold with sufficient estimability, scored on all 78 records. Any test \(C_D\) outside its cell's training range is flagged as extrapolation and reported, never dropped after seeing its outcome (Rev 1.4 §6.1). Flags are recomputed within every pipeline run against that run's own training ranges (observed or permuted, §5.3 step 2) and are **diagnostic only** — they never drop or add scored rows.
3. **Fold-level estimability (design-only conditions).** Per fold and cell, report \(G_c^{\mathrm{train}}\), \(D_c^{\mathrm{train}}\), \(W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}=\sum_{v\in c,\,\mathrm{train}}(C_D(v)-\overline C_{D,c}^{\mathrm{train}})^2\), and \(\sum_c W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}\); separation and convergence status are reported per fit as **fit diagnostics** (§4) and are never used as eligibility, scoring, or admissibility conditions (§5.7 V2). A cell contributes within-cell information only when \(G_c^{\mathrm{train}}\ge2\), \(D_c^{\mathrm{train}}\ge2\), and \(W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}>0\) — three conditions that are functions of the design and the \(C_D\) configuration alone, never of outcomes or fit results. A cell that fails these conditions in the audit is not scored, and its exclusion reason is listed (item 4(b)); under a permuted assignment the **declared audit-equality key must be reproduced** (§5.3 step 1): a scored fold-cell that loses support, **or a previously excluded fold-cell that becomes estimable**, makes the assignment inadmissible — no fold is ever silently scored with a zeroed slope, a reduced baseline, or a changed test population, and no excluded fold is silently added.
4. **Unequal scored-parent coverage.** \(|\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}|\) and its membership are properties of each realized dataset and can vary across datasets (§8 replications). Declared handling: (a) the statistic always uses each dataset's own \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}\) with equal weight per scored parent — no imputation of missing scored rows; (b) exclusion reasons per parent listed, **design-based reasons only** (cell absent from training, failed fold-cell estimability conditions); fit failures are not exclusions — they trigger the availability rule (§5.7 V5); (c) reports include per-fold and per-dataset coverage tables; under the §5.3 frozen-audit rule, within-dataset resample coverage is constant by construction and is reported as that fixed value (the coverage-distribution requirement applies across §8 replications, each with its own audit). No parent is silently added to or removed from the estimand.

## 4. Fitting, regularization, convergence (Rev 1.4 item iv)

1. **Models (Rev 1.4 §6.1, verbatim):** baseline \(\operatorname{logit}\Pr(Y_{v,r}=1)=\alpha_{c(v)}+\omega_r\); enriched \(\operatorname{logit}\Pr(Y_{v,r}=1)=\alpha_{c(v)}+\omega_r+\beta_C\,C_D(v)\). \(C_D\) enters on its **raw \((0,1]\) scale — no centering or rescaling**, so the meaning of \(\beta_C\) is fixed. \(\omega_r\) carries a **sum-to-zero (centered) constraint** over the 78 records; \(\alpha_c\) is unconstrained per exact cell. Reference/contrast coding is fixed by this sentence before any fit.
2. **Estimation.** Maximum likelihood via iteratively reweighted least squares (Fisher scoring), identical settings for both models. **Convergence criterion (declared in v1.4 — GPT v1.3 adjudication, item 5a).** At each iterate, let \(g_j\) denote the \(j\)-th component of the gradient of the model's active objective at that iterate — the log-likelihood \(\ell_m\) of item 3's penalized form, or the Firth-penalized objective \(\ell^{\mathrm{Firth}}_m\) when the item 3 correction is active — in the constraint-satisfying parameterization of item 1, and \(\gamma_j\) the \(j\)-th parameter component. The fit is **converged** when the sup-norm of the per-component relative gradient, \(\displaystyle \max_j\,\lvert g_j\rvert\,/\,\max(1,\lvert\gamma_j\rvert)\), is \(\le 10^{-8}\), evaluated identically for both models and for both objectives; otherwise the fit stops after at most 200 iterations, whichever comes first. Nonconvergence is reported and triggers item 3's rule as before.
3. **Separation rule (prespecified, deterministic given training data; executability specified in v1.3 — GPT correction c).** For every fold, the enriched model is fitted first under item 2's settings. **Trigger (evaluated identically in every fold and every resample, on the enriched model's fit — the only slope):** (i) the enriched fit did not converge under item 2's settings; or (ii) \(|\widehat\beta_C| > 50\); or (iii) the Wald standard error of \(\widehat\beta_C\) exceeds \(10^{3}\). If any trigger fires, **both models of that fold are refitted with the Firth-type Jeffreys-penalty correction, applied identically to both**: the penalized objective for model \(m \in \{0,1\}\) is
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{Firth}}_{m}(\gamma_{m}) \;=\; \ell_{m}(\gamma_{m}) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det \mathcal{I}_{m}(\gamma_{m}),
\]
where \(\ell_m\) is the model's log-likelihood over the fold's training rows, \(\mathcal{I}_m\) its Fisher information matrix at \(\gamma_m\), and \(\gamma_m\) the model's parameter vector in the **constraint-satisfying parameterization** declared in item 1 — \(\gamma_0 = (\alpha_c, \omega_r)\) with the sum-to-zero constraint on \(\omega_r\) enforced inside the penalized fit, \(\gamma_1 = (\alpha_c, \omega_r, \beta_C)\). The trigger is evaluated on the enriched model's slope; **the correction is applied to both models of the fold**, so both use the same estimation principle within the fold. Refit settings are identical to item 2 (item 2's declared convergence criterion, at most 200 iterations). The fold is flagged "separation-handled", with the trigger that fired recorded. The rule depends only on training-fold data and is applied identically inside every fold and every resample; the count of separation-handled folds and the triggers are reported per dataset and per resample. No other regularization is used; there is no tuning outside the training fold. **If either Firth-penalized refit still fails to converge under the item 2 criterion, or its information matrix \(\mathcal I_m\) is not useably positive definite at any iterate — operationally: the declared linear-algebra routine fails to factorize it, or the computed \(\log\det\mathcal I_m\) is non-finite — no fallback exists:** no assignment is discarded, no substitute procedure or silent regularization is used, and the **entire confirmatory test is declared unavailable for that dataset** (§5.7 V5(b); a singular or non-evaluable information matrix is a declared fitting failure — GPT v1.3 adjudication, item 5a). The linear-algebra routine and its tolerances are declared in the GO-time computational plan **before** any evaluation.
4. **Common slope as declared assumption.** The common \(\beta_C\) is an explicit modelling assumption (Rev 1.4 §6.1), never replaced after seeing outcomes. The **cell-specific-slope sensitivity** \(\beta_{C,c}\) is run exactly as Rev 1.4 §6.1 declares — same fold rules, same scored sets; a cell contributes a slope only when \(W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}>0\); the sensitivity is reported only when at least two cells contribute; and per GPT's adjudication the report states **how many cells are slope-estimable and each estimable cell's individual support** (per-cell training version counts, \(W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}\), fitted-slope stability diagnostics), never a bare pass/fail flag. Sensitivity only; divergence from the common-slope primary is reported, not resolved; no data-driven promotion.
5. **The baseline is never substituted.** No reduced baseline replaces the primary exact-cell baseline in the primary analysis (Rev 1.4 §6.1 "no pooling or silent fallback"). Any reduced-form comparison is a separately labelled secondary and is never pooled with primary results.

## 5. Confirmatory inference under overlapping LOPO folds (Rev 1.4 item i)

### 5.1 Why the naive test is excluded

LOPO training sets overlap heavily (folds \(g\) and \(g'\) differ only by the two held-out parents' rows), so per-parent scores are dependent and a paired t-test treating them as independent is not accepted (Rev 1.4 §6.1). The confirmatory calibration must therefore come from a randomization structure that preserves the outcome-side dependence — declared in §§5.2–5.4, with its validity conditions and declared limits in §5.7.

### 5.2 Null hypothesis and resampling unit (declared)

- **Null hypothesis \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\) (composite, restated in v1.2).** Conditional on the design — the eligible cells \((q,Z,k,\ell)\), the parent clustering, and the canonical record structure — and on the observed outcome vectors and the observed within-cell \(C_D\) multisets, the within-cell **configuration** of \(C_D\) labels is distributed as a uniform draw over its distinct reassignments (the reference distribution of §5.3). Its interpretive reading: \(C_D\) carries **no incremental predictive content beyond the exact cell through any channel** — not the direct coefficient \(\beta_C\) alone, but also latent parent-quality prediction through \(\theta_g\) (the N3 channel) and proxying of unresolved placement \(\xi_v\) (the N4-b channel), or any other coupling of the supplied exposure to the outcomes given the cell. This is the predeclared confirmatory null corresponding to the recorded B1 target; it is **stronger than the direct-channel null \(\beta_C = 0\) alone**, and the test is not claimed valid for the narrower null (§5.7 V1, V4).
- **What moves and what stays attached (v1.2, explicit — GPT finding 1).** The resampling unit is the **within-cell \(C_D\) label, applied at the version level**. For a permutation \(\pi\) (a product of independent uniform within-cell permutations), the **permuted exposure supplied to the predictive models** is
\[
C_D^\pi(v) \;=\; C_D(\pi_c(v)) \qquad (v \in \text{cell } c).
\]
It is a **permuted exposure, not a recomputed diagnostic quantity**: the raw failure vector \(f(v)\), the unresolved vector \(u(v)\), the parent identity, the cell membership \(c(v)\), the version identity, and the full 78-record outcome vector \((Y_{v,r})_r\) **remain attached to their original version and are never moved, regenerated, or recomputed**. Because the cell fixes \(k\) and \(\ell\), the swap is equivalently a swap of the \(M\) values within the cell — but even so \(C_D^\pi(v)\) must **never be presented as though it were recomputed from the version's actual failures**: it is not a new diagnostic table, and the models receive the supplied exposure \(C_D^\pi\) and nothing else about \(v\) changes. Outcome vectors keep their full within-parent dependence; parents stay clustered; records and cells are untouched; the null reference therefore inherits the outcome-side dependence automatically — no fold-independence assumption is used anywhere.
- **What the permutation deliberately breaks (GPT ruling).** Permuting the supplied exposure **severs** the association between concentration and latent parent quality (the \(C_D\)–\(\theta_g\) relationship, the N3 channel) and between concentration and unresolved placement (the \(C_D\)–\(\xi_v\) relationship, the N4-b channel). These associations are **part of the tested null, not preserved structure**: under \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\) they carry no outcome-relevant content, and the reference distribution is generated with them broken. The test therefore has power against precisely those channels; it is not a procedure that preserves the original diagnostic data's associations.
- **Status of the assumption (declared; v1.1 content, v1.2 pointer).** The exchangeability of the within-cell label configuration is a **substantive null assumption, not an automatic property of clustered outcomes**: preserving the outcome vectors preserves within-parent outcome dependence, but it does **not** preserve (and under the null is not supposed to preserve) the parent-level structure of the concentration labels. §5.7 (V1, V4) defends the assumption for the declared DGP catalogue of Rev 1.4 §2.5 and §7.2 — including why common parent offsets (\(\kappa_F\theta_g\), \(\kappa_U\varphi_g\)) and parent-level outcome shifts do not break it, and why N3 (\(\gamma_s^F\) nonconstant) and N4-b (\(\beta_{u\to y} \ne 0\) with within-cell \(\xi\)–\(C_D\) correlation) break it **by construction** — and states the limits under which the test must be **declared unavailable for the intended target** rather than used. The exactness claimed anywhere in this plan is **conditional** (on the design-support event and on the exchangeability assumption) — it is a **conditional randomization test**, never "unconditionally exact" (§5.7 V3). It holds under N1-type nulls (\(\beta_C = 0\) with no channel coupling \(C_D\) to outcomes within cells) and fails by construction under N3/N4-b-type processes; detecting exactly such violations of \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\) is how the test detects incremental prediction. A rejection therefore cannot, by itself, separate the channel (§5.6); the N-control evaluations of §§8–10 characterize the rejection rate under each declared scenario so that interpretation is calibrated, and the N3 reading rule (§10, Rev 1.4 §7.2) governs the interpretation of a gain that arises through the parent-quality channel.

### 5.3 Test procedure (frozen once this plan is accepted)

0. **Design-only audit and frozen target population (step 0, before any permutation; GPT finding 2, v1.1 adjudication; complete-audit recording added in v1.3).** On the observed data, run the §3 design-only audit: the eligible cells; the scored parents \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}\); the per-fold test rows; and the per-fold-cell estimability conditions (\(G_c^{\mathrm{train}}\ge2\), \(D_c^{\mathrm{train}}\ge2\), \(W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}>0\) for every scored fold-cell). These sets are functions of the **design and the \(C_D\) configuration only** — they never involve outcomes, fit results, or scores — and they are **frozen for the entire test**: the observed statistic and every permutation value are computed on exactly these scored rows, for exactly these folds. **The complete audit output \(\mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\) — including the excluded fold-cells and their decisions — is recorded and frozen** (v1.3); admissibility is defined by reproducing its **declared selection key** exactly (step 1, v1.4). Then compute \(\Delta LL_{\mathrm{obs}} = T(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\) by the frozen pipeline of §§3–4 (both models refit in every fold; the §4 item 3 separation rule applied identically), where \(T(b)\) denotes the pipeline's \(\Delta LL\) when assignment \(b\)'s exposures are supplied and \(b_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is the observed assignment.
1. **Admissible space: \(\Omega\) and \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) (step 1; GPT findings 2 of the v1.1 and v1.2 adjudications).** Let \(\Omega\) be the set of all distinct within-cell reassignments of the observed \(C_D\) values (\(\lvert\Omega\rvert = \prod_c n_c!/\prod_m n_{c,m}!\), §5.4). Let \(\mathcal{A}\) denote the **complete design-only audit function**: applied to an assignment \(b\), it returns the full audit output — the eligible cells, the scored parents \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}\), the per-fold test rows, **the scored and the excluded fold-cells together with their decisions**, and every eligibility decision of §3 — all computed from the design and the \(C_D\) configuration under \(b\), never from outcomes. The admissible space is
\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}} \;=\; \bigl\{\, b \in \Omega \;:\; \mathcal{A}(b) = \mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}}) \,\bigr\},
\]
the assignments that **reproduce the observed selection event exactly**: every frozen scored fold-cell retains the required predictor support **and** every fold-cell excluded by the observed audit remains excluded under the same conditions — an assignment under which a previously excluded fold-cell becomes estimable is **inadmissible**, exactly as one under which a scored fold-cell loses support is inadmissible. (v1.2 conditioned only on the survival of the scored fold-cells; that did not preserve the full selection event — corrected per GPT's v1.2 adjudication, 2026-11-14.) **The audit-equality key (declared in v1.4 — GPT v1.3 adjudication, blocking finding 1).** Equality \(\mathcal{A}(b) = \mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is evaluated on a declared ordered key consisting exactly of the **selection decisions** — nothing more, nothing less. **Included in the key:** (i) each full-population cell's \(B1\) eligibility decision; (ii) every LOPO fold-cell's scored/excluded status together with its recorded exclusion reason; (iii) the scored-parent membership \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}\); (iv) the exact scored test-row identifier set per fold; and (v) every other design-only decision that determines the analysis population — any such decision added at GO time must be declared in the GO-time computational plan **before** evaluation. **Excluded from the key** (reported diagnostics, never elements of the selection event): the numerical value of \(W_{C,c}^{\mathrm{train}}\) (only its positivity decision is a selection decision, via (ii)'s reason), other numerical support/variation diagnostics that do not change a decision, fitted-model results, scores, extrapolation flags, and numerical concentration ranges. Two assignments are admissible-equivalent iff their declared keys are identical; admissibility is \(\mathrm{key}(b) = \mathrm{key}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\). This conditions on the complete selection event **without conditioning on irrelevant numerical details** — an over-conditioned key could shrink \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) toward the observed assignment alone and destroy the reference distribution; an under-conditioned key could admit assignments that change the analysis population (GPT, 2026-11-14, binding). **Membership in \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is determined by design and predictor quantities only** (fixed folds, cell membership, parent counts, concentration-variation gates). **Prohibited as membership conditions:** whether a model fit converged; whether separation occurred (the §4 item 3 rule is applied downstream, identically to every assignment — never as a filter); whether an outcome pattern made a fit numerically unstable; whether an assignment produced a favourable or unfavourable score. The plan reports \(\lvert\Omega\rvert\), \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\), and \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert/\lvert\Omega\rvert\), and **confirms and reports that the observed assignment belongs to \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\)** (by construction — the audit is run on it — the confirmation is still explicitly reported). **When \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert = 1\), the only admissible assignment is the observed one; the reference distribution is degenerate and no rejection is possible at any level — reported as such, never hidden.** No assignment is silently discarded: in the exact branch, inadmissible assignments are excluded by this declared design rule with the \(\lvert\Omega\rvert\), \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\) accounting reported; in the Monte Carlo branch they are rejected by the declared rejection sampler with the acceptance rate reported (§5.4). Admissible assignments are **never** discarded: a fitting failure that the deterministic §4 item 3 rule does not resolve makes the whole test unavailable (§5.7 V5(b)).
2. **Permutation resamples (step 2; GPT findings 1 and 3).** For each resample \(b\): independently within each eligible cell \(c\), draw a uniform permutation of the cell's versions and reassign their \(C_D\) values accordingly, producing the permuted exposure \(C_D^{(b)}(v) = C_D^\pi(v) = C_D(\pi_c(v))\) — the \(C_D^\pi\) notation of §5.2 (a uniform raw permutation of a cell's versions induces the uniform law on that cell's distinct reassignments, since each distinct arrangement is realized by the same number of raw permutations). **The permutation is applied to the entire primary sample before the LOPO training/test partitions are constructed** (GPT, 2026-11-14): the partitions are then built exactly as in the observed run — and they are identical to it, because partitions depend only on parent membership and cell structure, which never move. Within every resample, all **fitted parameters** are recomputed: both models are refit in every LOPO fold on the same training rows under the §4 rules, including the deterministic separation rule, which applies identically in the observed and permuted pipelines; no fitted quantity is carried across resamples. Extrapolation flags are **recomputed within the resample** against that resample's permuted training ranges and are **diagnostic only**: they never drop or add scored rows (§3 item 2).
   - **Monte Carlo comparison set (v1.5 — GPT v1.4 adjudication).** Under the Monte Carlo branch (§5.4), the reference distribution is formed from the declared **comparison set**: the observed assignment \(b_{\mathrm{obs}}\) plus the \(R_{\mathrm{perm}}\) accepted sampled assignments. The **identical complete fitting procedure** — the frozen pipeline of §§3–4, including the §4 item 3 separation rule and its declared failure rule — is applied to **every member** of the comparison set, observed and sampled alike; no member's fit status is assessed by any other or weaker rule, and a fitting failure is never silently discarded or replaced (§5.7 V5(b)). Under \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\), conditional on the design, the outcomes, the within-cell exposure multisets, and the audit-selection event, the comparison-set members are independent uniform draws on \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) (§5.4); the §5.3 step 4 Monte Carlo p-value's super-uniformity is therefore **conditional on the symmetric availability event** that every comparison-set member fits successfully (§5.7 V3, V5(b)), and **nothing about assignments outside the comparison set is claimed or verified**.
3. **Invariant target population (step 3; v1.1 content, tightened).** The scored sets, fold definitions, and audited row sets are fixed by the **step 0 design-only audit** (§3 item 3 conditions) and are held **identical** across the observed statistic and every permutation resample (a permutation-invariant target population): the observed \(\Delta LL\) and every null value are computed on the same scored rows for the same folds, so the null is conditional on the same target population as the observed statistic. (Scored-set construction from changed data occurs only in the §8 synthetic replications, where each replication is its own dataset and its own step 0 design-only audit fixes its sets.)
4. **Decision rule (step 4; GPT finding 5).** The permutation reference distribution is generated by the same fitting procedure and **need not be centred at zero** — fitting an extra slope on a null predictor typically shifts out-of-sample \(\Delta LL\) slightly negative, so an absolute-value comparison around zero would be anti-conservative. The two-sided p-value is therefore built from the two tails of the reference distribution itself, **by branch**:
   - **Exact-enumeration branch** (§5.4; assignments \(b \in \Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\), each counted once):
   \[
   p_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{\#\{b \in \Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}:T(b)\ge T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert},\qquad
   p_{\mathrm{L}}=\frac{\#\{b \in \Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}:T(b)\le T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert}
   \]
   with **no \(+1\) correction**: the denominator is the complete admissible-assignment count, which already contains the observed assignment. This is per GPT's ruling (2026-11-14); the Monte Carlo \(+1\) convention is **not** carried into exact enumeration.
   - **Monte Carlo branch** (§5.4; \(R_{\mathrm{perm}}\) accepted assignments plus the observed one):
   \[
   p_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1+\#\{b:\,T(b)\ge T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{R_{\mathrm{perm}}+1},\qquad
   p_{\mathrm{L}}=\frac{1+\#\{b:\,T(b)\le T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{R_{\mathrm{perm}}+1},
   \]
   with the \(+1\) retained (GPT, 2026-11-14).
   In **both** modes, ties are counted conservatively in both tails (\(\ge\) and \(\le\) respectively, so every tie contributes to each tail). The two-sided p-value is
   \[
   p=\min\bigl(1,\;2\min(p_{\mathrm{L}},\,p_{\mathrm{R}})\bigr),
   \]
   reject \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\) at \(\alpha = 0.05\) when \(p \le 0.05\). This is the **single confirmatory test** of the plan. The report gives \(p_{\mathrm{L}}\), \(p_{\mathrm{R}}\), \(p\), and the reference-distribution median of \(\Delta LL\). The one-sided positive reading used by the N4-a control (§8) is \(p_{\mathrm{R}}\) with its own declared threshold; the two-sided and one-sided uses are separate predeclared procedures.

### 5.4 Enumeration and sampling modes (v1.2: admissible-space conditioning; v1.3: audit-equality membership)

Let \(\lvert\Omega\rvert=\prod_c n_c!/\prod_m n_{c,m}!\), where \(n_c\) is a cell's version count and \(n_{c,m}\) the multiplicity of each distinct \(C_D\) value in it.

- **Exact-enumeration branch.** If \(\lvert\Omega\rvert \le 10^{6}\), enumerate all distinct within-cell reassignments; each is checked for \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) membership by the **design-only audit-equality check** \(\mathcal{A}(b) = \mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\) on the declared key (§5.3 step 1) (no fitting); the reference distribution is uniform on \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and the p-value uses the \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\) denominator (§5.3 step 4). Cost: at most \(10^{6}\) design checks plus at most \(10^{6}\) pipeline evaluations per dataset. This branch is preferred for its **determinism** — no sampling noise and an exact admissible-space denominator, whereas the Monte Carlo branch has declared resolution \(1/(R_{\mathrm{perm}}+1)\). The bound is a **declared practicality threshold, not a measured one**: the GO-time computational plan (§8 item iii) must demonstrate that it is feasible before the enumeration branch is used; if it is not, the plan falls to the Monte Carlo branch.
- **Monte Carlo branch.** Otherwise, the reference distribution is sampled **uniformly from \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) by rejection sampling** (GPT, 2026-11-14): draw independent uniform within-cell reassignments (uniform on \(\Omega\)); accept an assignment iff \(\mathcal{A}(b) = \mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\) on the declared key (§5.3 step 1) — a **design check only**, so rejected draws never reach the pipeline and never consume a fit; collect \(R_{\mathrm{perm}} = 9{,}999\) accepted assignments (plus the observed one). Conditioning a uniform draw on \(\Omega\) on the audit-equality event yields the uniform law on \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\). The total draw count, acceptance count, and acceptance rate are reported. A **declared** draw cap of \(10^{8}\) design checks applies: if fewer than \(R_{\mathrm{perm}}\) assignments are accepted within the cap, the Monte Carlo branch is declared infeasible and the confirmatory test is **declared unavailable** (§5.7 V5(c)) — no silent degradation, no substitution. Accepted assignments may repeat when \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert \le R_{\mathrm{perm}}\); the resulting granularity is reported (§5.5).
  - **Availability condition (v1.5 — GPT v1.4 adjudication).** The §5.3 step 4 Monte Carlo p-value's super-uniformity holds **conditional on the symmetric availability event** — the same deterministic fitting and availability rule (§4 item 3; §5.7 V5(b)) applied to every member of the declared comparison set (§5.3 step 2) — and under **independent uniform draws** from \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\); conditional on availability, the comparison-set members are uniform over the fit-available subset \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}^{\mathrm{fit}}=\{b\in\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}:F(b)=1\}\), and the branch does **not** claim that unsampled admissible assignments have been verified fit-available (§5.7 V3). The GO-time computational plan includes a **design-only** feasibility assessment of the expected acceptance rate — from the design combinatorics (\(\lvert\Omega\rvert\), the cell version counts, the audit key's selectivity), with **no outcome-dependent computation** — before sampling begins; the declared cap rule above is the binding consequence if the acceptance rate makes the cap impractical: the test run is unavailable (§5.7 V5(c)), and the cap is never relaxed (§5.7 V7).
- **Seeds and RNG documentation (v1.5 — GPT v1.4 adjudication).** Predeclared seed 20261114; all seeds recorded in output. The GO-time computational plan documents the pseudorandom number generator and its use — algorithm, seeding, stream usage, and draw accounting. The seed is a **reproducibility device**: the formal Monte Carlo guarantee is with respect to the stated sampling model (independent uniform draws, conditioned on the audit-equality event and on availability — §5.3 step 2, §5.7 V3), **not** a guarantee conditional on one deterministic seed. Designing this structure is within the present authorization; executing it is not (§12).

### 5.5 Null granularity (v1.2: bootstrap removed)

- The number of distinct reference-distribution statistic values is reported. When it is small — in particular when \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\) is small — the p-value's resolution is coarse and the test is conservative; this is stated in the report — no silent switch to a parametric or asymptotic test.
- **No secondary uncertainty summary is predeclared.** The v1.1 labelled parent-level bootstrap percentile interval is **removed** in v1.2 (GPT ruling 2026-11-14: removal preferred over the cluster-preserving repair). No bootstrap interval, no other resampling interval, and no asymptotic interval is predeclared for \(\Delta LL\); any future addition requires a pre-registered amendment through the review chain **before** any evaluation (§7 item 3).

### 5.6 Interpretation of a rejection (channels kept distinct; sign-specific in v1.3 — GPT v1.2 adjudication, blocking finding 3)

The two-sided test of §5.3 step 4 is retained (Rev 1.4 §6.1 requires two-sided), but its interpretation is **sign-specific**. The report identifies which tail drove the two-sided decision (§5.3 step 4, §11(d)):

- **Upper-tail rejection** (\(p_{\mathrm{R}} \le p_{\mathrm{L}}\), so \(p_{\mathrm{R}}\) drives \(p\)): the observed concentration assignment produces an unusually **high** predictive gain relative to the randomized reference. This can support **positive incremental prediction** under the declared target. Even so, the reading is specific to the **predeclared common-slope P-pred model**, the **fixed development bank**, and the **held-out-parent scoring rule**; it is not proof of a direct concentration mechanism, and the channel remains unresolved without §§8–10.
- **Lower-tail rejection** (\(p_{\mathrm{L}} < p_{\mathrm{R}}\), so \(p_{\mathrm{L}}\) drives \(p\)): the observed concentration assignment produces an unusually **poor** predictive gain relative to the randomized reference. This **does not establish positive incremental predictive information**; it indicates an adverse or otherwise unusual relationship under the specified predictive procedure and **requires its own separate interpretation** — it is never reported as evidence that concentration improves prediction.

In both cases, rejection establishes only that the observed assignment is unusual in the corresponding tail of the reference distribution of the predeclared statistic. **It does not by itself attribute a channel.** The reading — direct concentration information, latent parent-quality prediction through \(\theta_g\) (the N3 case), proxying of omitted unresolved-placement information (the N4-b case), or leakage/misspecification — is resolved only jointly with the N-control reports of §§8–10. No mechanism-level attribution from the gain alone (Rev 1.4 §7.2). In particular, under N3-structured generating processes the exchangeability null fails **by construction** (within-cell \(C_D\) proxies \(\theta_g\)); that behaviour is reported under the N3 control and its declared interpretation, not hidden and not scored as a procedure failure. Failure modes **outside** the declared catalogue are a declared interpretive limit, not a silently ignored case (§5.7 V6(ii)).

### 5.7 Validity conditions and honest failure modes (introduced v1.2; V2 and V4 amended in v1.3 per GPT's v1.2 adjudication)

The confirmatory procedure of §§5.2–5.5 is a **conditional randomization test**. Its validity rests on the following declared conditions; none of them is treated as automatic, and the test must not be used for a target for which they cannot be defended.

- **V1 — Exchangeability (the substantive assumption).** Under \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\), within each eligible cell the \(C_D\) labels are generated by an exchangeable mechanism independent of the outcome vectors given the exact cell structure. **Defense for the declared catalogue** (Rev 1.4 §2.5, §7.2): (a) conditional on the exact totals \((k,\ell)\), common parent offsets \(\kappa_F\theta_g\) and \(\kappa_U\varphi_g\) cancel from the Fisher-type conditional allocation law — they affect cell-membership probabilities but not the within-cell label law (Rev 1.4 §2.5, verbatim in substance), and a common parent intercept alone "is not N3 because it cancels after conditioning on \((k,\ell)\)" (Rev 1.4 §7.2); (b) under the null-compatible settings (\(\beta_C = 0\), \(\beta_{u\to y} = 0\), parent effects either absent — \(\Sigma = 0\) — or acting only through channels that cancel from the within-cell allocation law), versions are conditionally i.i.d. in their \(C_D\) draws given the cell totals, so the within-cell label law is exchangeable and, conditioned on the realized within-cell multiset, uniform over its distinct rearrangements — the §§5.3–5.4 reference is exactly this law further conditioned on the outcome-independent design-support event (V2); (c) outcome vectors stay attached, so parent-level outcome dependence — including any common outcome shift — is preserved exactly and is orthogonal to the within-cell label law, so labels \(\perp\) outcomes \(\mid\) cell continues to hold. The assumption **fails by construction** under N3 (\(\gamma_s^F\) nonconstant couples the labels to \(\theta_g\)) and under N4-b (\(\beta_{u\to y}\ne0\) with within-cell \(\xi\)–\(C_D\) correlation) — the designed detection target. **Limits:** the defense is specific to the declared catalogue; version-level exchangeability is not implied by clustered outcomes; and if it cannot be defended for the intended null, the test is **declared unavailable for that target** — no silent switch to a different resampling scheme, no reinterpretation against the narrower null. This plan tests \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\) as defined in §5.2, not merely \(\beta_C = 0\).
- **V2 — Outcome-independence of the conditioning event (amended in v1.3: full selection event; key wording v1.4).** \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) membership is determined by the **complete design-only audit output** \(\mathcal{A}(b)\) — fixed folds, cell membership, parent counts, concentration-variation gates, and the resulting scored **and excluded** fold-cells — compared for equality with the observed audit output (\(\mathcal{A}(b) = \mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\), §5.3 step 1). **Equality is evaluated on the declared audit-equality key of §5.3 step 1** — selection decisions only; numerical diagnostics that do not determine selection, fitted-model results, scores, and extrapolation flags are excluded from the key and remain reported diagnostics (GPT, 2026-11-14, binding). Convergence, separation, outcome-pattern instability, and score favourability remain prohibited as membership conditions. Because \(\mathcal{A}\) is outcome-independent, the conditioning event \(\{\mathcal{A}(b) = \mathcal{A}(b_{\mathrm{obs}})\}\) is outcome-independent, so conditioning on it preserves the uniform reference under V1 (the standard conditional-randomization argument): the conditional null law on \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) remains uniform, and the observed assignment belongs to \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) by construction (confirmed and reported, §5.3 step 1). Conditioning on the **full selection event** — not merely the survival of the scored fold-cells — is what makes the reference distribution correspond to the same selection event and target population as the observed statistic (GPT, 2026-11-14, binding).
- **V3 — Conditional scope, not superpopulation.** The test is a **conditional randomization test**: its validity is conditional on (i) the observed design-support event (observed assignment \(\in \Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\)) and (ii) V1. It is **never described as unconditionally exact**; no superpopulation or across-dataset guarantee is claimed; the reference distribution is the within-dataset randomization law, conditional as stated. Each §8 synthetic replication instantiates the same conditional structure on its own dataset. **Monte Carlo availability (v1.5 — GPT v1.4 adjudication).** Under the Monte Carlo branch the p-value's super-uniformity is additionally **conditional on the symmetric availability event** \(A=\{F(b)=1\text{ for every member of the comparison set (§5.3 step 2)}\}\), where \(F(b)=1\) denotes that the complete fitting procedure — §§3–4, including the §4 item 3 rule and its declared failure rule — succeeds for assignment \(b\). Because the same deterministic fitting and availability rule is applied to the observed assignment and every sampled assignment, conditioning on \(A\) preserves exchangeability: conditional on \(A\), the comparison-set members are uniform over the fit-available subset \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}^{\mathrm{fit}}=\{b\in\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}:F(b)=1\}\). The Monte Carlo test **does not claim** to have enumerated, or verified the fit-availability of, any assignment outside its comparison set; the exact branch is the only mode in which every admissible assignment's fitting is evaluated. A failed sampled fit is never silently discarded or replaced (§5.7 V5(b)).
- **V4 — Parent-level uncertainty: conditional target vs parent-population target (rewritten in v1.3; GPT v1.2 adjudication, blocking finding 2).** Two distinct inferential targets must be kept apart:
  - **Conditional target:** is the observed concentration assignment unusually predictive, conditional on **these particular parents, outcomes, cells, and exposure multisets**?
  - **Parent-population target:** does concentration provide predictive information that **generalizes across independently developed parent implementations**?
  This plan's confirmatory test addresses the **conditional target only**. Under the exchangeability assumption V1 and the design-support conditioning V2, the p-value quantifies how unusual the observed assignment's predictiveness is **within the observed configuration space** — given the realized parents and their outcome vectors, the realized cells, and the realized exposure multisets. The permutation does **not** resample (or preserve) the parent-level structure of the exposure labels, and therefore it **does not establish the parent-population target**: no claim is made that the gain generalizes to independently developed parents beyond the observed sample, and none may be inferred from this test alone. **Relation to Rev 1.4 §6.1:** §6.1 requires the plan to predeclare the *parent-level uncertainty procedure* for the confirmatory test of \(\Delta LL\) on the observed sample — "including refitting within each resample/permutation, handling of unequal scored-parent coverage, and multiplicity" — and names "bootstrap **or** permutation with within-resample refitting, and the resampling unit". This plan **meets that requirement's conditional inferential component only, and does not provide parent-population uncertainty** (v1.4 — GPT v1.3 adjudication, blocking finding 2): the resampling unit is declared (§5.2), refitting within every resample is predeclared (§5.3 step 2), scored-parent coverage is frozen and reported with equal per-parent weighting (§3, §5.3 step 0), and multiplicity is predeclared (§7). The procedure preserves outcome clustering and refits the LOPO pipeline, but it **neither resamples parent-level exposure structure nor estimates between-parent sampling uncertainty; the full parent-level requirement is not claimed satisfied**, and the limitation must not be represented as though it were. **If a parent-population claim is intended, this test is not valid for that target as specified:** such a claim requires a different, separately predeclared procedure (e.g. a parent-level resampling of exposures — removed from this plan in v1.2 — or a hierarchical superpopulation model), predeclared through the review chain **before** any evaluation; absent such a procedure, any parent-population uncertainty requirement that the intended target demands is **not met by this plan**, and the test is **declared unavailable for that target** — the conditional result is never silently re-advertised as population inference (GPT, 2026-11-14, binding).
- **V5 — Availability conditions.** The confirmatory test is **declared unavailable** for a dataset when: (a) the design-only audit (§5.3 step 0) yields no valid scored configuration, or the observed assignment fails the audit (it cannot by construction, but the confirmation is reported); (b) any fitting failure in the **applicable evaluation set** that is not resolved by the prespecified deterministic rule (§4 item 3, with the v1.3 deterministic trigger, penalized objective, and failure rule): the evaluation set is **branch-specific** (v1.5 — GPT v1.4 adjudication) — under the **exact-enumeration branch** it is every enumerated admissible assignment in \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\), all of which are evaluated, so a fitting failure in any one of them makes the test unavailable; under the **Monte Carlo branch** it is the declared comparison set of §5.3 step 2 — the observed assignment plus the \(R_{\mathrm{perm}}\) accepted sampled assignments — and a fitting failure in any member of that set makes that test run unavailable; the Monte Carlo branch does **not** claim, and cannot establish, that unsampled admissible assignments would also fit successfully (§5.7 V3): **no assignment is discarded in that case; the entire test is unavailable**; (c) the declared enumeration/sampling thresholds are infeasible without a predeclared fallback (§5.4). Unavailability is reported as such, with its reason; no p-value is claimed; \(\Delta LL\) remains descriptive.
- **V6 — Honest failure modes.** (i) Exchangeability fails in truth through a declared channel (N3/N4-b-like): rejection is **possible, not guaranteed** — its frequency under such alternatives is the test's **rejection rate / operating characteristic**, evaluated per declared scenario and depending on the scenario and effect strength (agreeing with §8(v); GPT, 2026-11-14); a rejection cannot by itself separate channels (§5.6). (ii) Exchangeability fails through a pathway **outside** the declared catalogue (e.g. parent-level label structure coupled to outcomes by an unmodelled channel): a rejection is then not interpretable as evidence of any declared channel; the N-controls cover the declared catalogue only — "not all possible solver populations" (Rev 1.4 §7.2) — a declared interpretive limit. (iii) \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\) small: the conditional reference is valid but coarse and conservative; discreteness and granularity are reported (§5.5). (iv) Fitting failure beyond the deterministic rule: unavailable (V5) — never silently dropped. (v) Ties: counted conservatively in both tails in both modes (§5.3 step 4). **The plan does not dictate the scientific answer in advance:** a rejection is evidence against \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\) as defined; a non-rejection is absence of evidence at the declared level; neither is converted into a mechanism claim (§5.6, §10).
- **V7 — No result-driven switching.** The admissibility definition, the branch rule, the p-value formulas, the sidednesses, the thresholds, and the interpretations are fixed by this text **before** any evaluation. No element may be changed after results are seen; amendments go through the review chain **before** any evaluation (§7 item 3). A failed condition leads to a declared unavailability — never to degradation, substitution, or post-hoc reinterpretation.

## 8. N4-a operationalisation (Rev 1.4 item vi; Amendment E sub-items (i)–(vi))

Until this plan is frozen, N4-a remains interpretive only (Rev 1.4 §7.2). Once frozen, N4-a is operationalised as follows.

**(i) Null reference procedure.** Data generated from the declared truth model (Rev 1.4 §2.5, §7.1, §7.2) under the N4-a settings: the N1 configuration — \(\Sigma\) the zero matrix (no parent effects: \(\sigma_\theta^2 = \sigma_{\theta\varphi} = \sigma_\varphi^2 = 0\), consistent with N3 being defined as N1 except \(\operatorname{Var}(\theta_g)>0\)), identity baseline shapes \(b^F_{q,s}, b^U_{q,s}\) constant across strata within each identity, \(\gamma^F = \gamma^U = 0\), \(\beta_{u\to y}=0\) — evaluated at **crossed anchors** \(t^*(q) \ne s^*(q)\) with **predeclared positive finite** \(\lambda \in \{0.5,\ 1.0,\ 2.0\}\) (weak, moderate, and strong decay \(e^{-\lambda\Delta}\) for integer-gap penalty profiles). This \(\lambda\) set is fixed by this sentence; it may be changed only by an amendment accepted through the review chain **before** any N4-a evaluation, never after results are seen. Anchor profiles, base shapes, and all generating constants are recorded before generation.

**(ii) Calibration statistic.** The calibration statistic is \(\Delta LL\) itself, computed by the frozen pipeline (§§3–5) on each generated dataset. Its finite-sample null variability is quantified by the **within-dataset conditional randomization distribution** of §§5.2–5.5 applied to that dataset. **The reference distribution is branch-specific (v1.6 — GPT v1.5 adjudication).** Under **exact enumeration**, when every assignment in that dataset's \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) (the full selection event, §5.3 step 1) is fit-available, it is uniform over \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\). Under **Monte Carlo**, the proposals are sampled independently and uniformly from \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\) (\(R_{\mathrm{perm}} = 9{,}999\) accepted assignments via the §5.4 rejection sampler, with recorded seeds); **conditional on the symmetric availability event**, the observed assignment and all accepted sampled assignments in the comparison set (§5.3 step 2) are distributed uniformly over \(\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}^{\mathrm{fit}}=\{b\in\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}:F(b)=1\}\) (§5.7 V3). If the applicable availability condition fails, the replication is unavailable and no p-value is claimed (§5.7 V5; §8(vii)) — §8(iv)'s availability-conditional probability bound and §8(vii)'s no-replacement rule are unchanged. The Monte Carlo procedure makes **no claim about the fitting availability of assignments outside its comparison set** (§5.7 V3, V5(b)). The same machinery that calibrates the real-data test is therefore the one audited under the null — there is no second, divergent calibration.

**(iii) Replication/resampling structure.** \(N_{\mathrm{rep}} = 100\) **independent replications** of the N4-a generating process per predeclared \(\lambda\) value. Each replication is **its own dataset**: it generates the full diagnostic tables and held-out outcomes, its **step 0 design-only audit** (§5.3, §3 item 3 conditions) fixes its own scored sets once per replication, and the complete LOPO pipeline computes \(\Delta LL_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and its within-dataset conditional randomization null on those sets. Both models are refit within every fold of every replication and within every accepted randomization assignment — the baseline fit count is \(N_{\mathrm{rep}}\times\widetilde R\times\lvert\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}\rvert\times 2\) models per \(\lambda\) value, where \(\widetilde R\) is that replication's pipeline-evaluation count — \(1+R_{\mathrm{perm}}\) in the Monte Carlo branch (the observed assignment plus the accepted draws) and \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\) in the exact branch (the observed assignment is itself admissible, so its statistic is one of the enumerated evaluations — no separate observed run; §5.4's cost statement counts \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}\rvert\) evaluations the same way) — and \(\lvert\mathcal G_{\mathrm{score}}\rvert\) is that replication's number of LOPO folds (§2, §3 item 1: one fold per scored parent). §4 item 3 separation-handled refits add up to two further fits per affected fold and are reported. Rejected rejection-sampler draws add design checks only, never model fits. The **computational plan demonstrating this is tractable must be stated at GO time; it is not asserted here** (the specification's finite-sum/tractability honesty carries over).

**(iv) Reproducibility criterion (v1.1: sidedness explicit; v1.2: discreteness bound added).** The per-replication criterion is **one-sided positive** at per-replication level \(0.025\): replication \(j\) yields **positive exceedance** when its one-sided randomization p-value \(p_{\mathrm{R}}^{(j)}\) — computed by the §5.3 step 4 rules on that replication's own within-dataset admissible reference (exact branch: denominator \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}^{(j)}\rvert\), no \(+1\); Monte Carlo branch: \(+1\) formula) — satisfies \(p_{\mathrm{R}}^{(j)} \le 0.025\), equivalently \(\Delta LL_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(j)}\) lies in the upper \(2.5\%\) tail of that reference. One-sided positive is the declared sidedness because, under N4-a, only a **positive** gain can indicate leakage or misspecification — a negative \(\Delta LL\) cannot. This criterion is deliberately **not** the §5.3 two-sided rule (which answers the real-data confirmatory question); the two-sided and one-sided uses are separate predeclared procedures. The gain is declared **reproducible beyond the predeclared finite-sample null variability** when the exceedance count \(K\) over the \(N_{\mathrm{rep}}\) replications satisfies
\[
K \;\ge\; c_{\mathrm{crit}} \;=\; \min\bigl\{K : \Pr\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(N_{\mathrm{rep}},\,0.025) \ge K\bigr) \le 0.05\bigr\}.
\]
With the predeclared \(N_{\mathrm{rep}} = 100\) this evaluates to \(c_{\mathrm{crit}} = 6\) (exact binomial tail \(\Pr(\mathrm{Bin}(100,0.025)\ge6) \approx 0.04 \le 0.05\), while \(\Pr(\ge5) \approx 0.11 > 0.05\)) — hand arithmetic under the §8-style illustrative exception of the specification; the exact value is confirmed by exact binomial enumeration at evaluation time under a GO, before generation, and recorded.

**Discreteness bound (v1.2; GPT ruling 6).** Under the calibrated null, the per-replication exceedance probability is **bounded above by \(0.025\), not necessarily equal to it**: the admissible reference distribution is discrete, ties are counted conservatively in the tail (§5.3 step 4), and the randomization p-value's null law is super-uniform — \(\Pr(p_{\mathrm{R}} \le a) \le a\) for every \(a\) — in both the exact and Monte Carlo branches. The binomial upper-tail calibration at \(0.025\) is therefore **conservative**: declaring leakage requires at least the nominal evidence, possibly more. Per-replication discreteness (the realized tail count and \(\lvert\Omega_{\mathrm{adm}}^{(j)}\rvert\)) is reported. **Availability condition (v1.5 — GPT v1.4 adjudication).** In the Monte Carlo branch this super-uniformity is **conditional on the symmetric availability event** (§5.3 step 2, §5.4, §5.7 V3 and V5(b)) and assumes **independent uniform draws** from the admissible space; and the per-replication bound \(\Pr(p_{\mathrm{R}}^{(j)} \le 0.025) \le 0.025\) holds **conditional on replication \(j\)'s being available** (§5.7 V5). The §8(vii) rules are unchanged: any unavailable replication makes that \(\lambda\)'s exceedance test unavailable, and no unavailable replication is replaced.

**(v) Evidence threshold.** \(K \ge c_{\mathrm{crit}}\) at a predeclared \(\lambda\) value constitutes, per Rev 1.4 §7.2 N4-a and Amendment D, **evidence of leakage or model misspecification under this control** — a reproducible gain beyond finite-sample null variability where the declared truth model contains no \(C_D\) pathway. \(K < c_{\mathrm{crit}}\) supports no such declaration; **an isolated positive estimate can arise by chance under a correctly specified null and is not, by itself, evidence of a data leak** (Amendment D wording, restated as binding). The empirical exceedance rate is additionally reported for every null scenario evaluated, with its role stated per scenario (v1.3 — GPT correction b): **N1, N2, and N4-a are the null-calibration scenarios under their declared settings** — their rates are reported against the nominal \(0.025\) as calibration diagnostics, expected at or below \(0.025\) under the §8(iv) bound; **N3 and N4-b deliberately violate \(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\)** — their rates are reported as **rejection rates / operating characteristics**, never as null calibration.

**(vi) Multiplicity (v1.1: full procedure; v1.2: family restated).** The N4-a family is the set of exceedance tests at the predeclared \(\lambda\) values \(\{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}\) — three family members, each built from the \(N_{\mathrm{rep}}\) per-replication one-sided \(p_{\mathrm{R}} \le 0.025\) criteria of §8(iv). Per \(\lambda\), the count \(K_\lambda\) converts to an exact p-value
\[
p_\lambda \;=\; \Pr\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(N_{\mathrm{rep}},\,0.025) \ge K_\lambda\bigr),
\]
computed exactly by binomial enumeration at evaluation time **before** any generation, from the predeclared \(N_{\mathrm{rep}} = 100\) and per-replication level \(0.025\) — no estimation, no rounding. Holm's step-down procedure is then applied at familywise \(\alpha = 0.05\): order \(p_{(1)} \le p_{(2)} \le p_{(3)}\); reject the hypothesis with \(p_{(1)}\) if \(p_{(1)} \le 0.05/3\); if and only if it was rejected, reject \(p_{(2)}\) if \(p_{(2)} \le 0.05/2\); if and only if that too was rejected, reject \(p_{(3)}\) if \(p_{(3)} \le 0.05\). A leakage/misspecification declaration names the \(\lambda\) value(s) at which rejection occurred. If \(N_{\mathrm{rep}}\) or the per-replication level is ever amended, the same formulas recompute \(c_{\mathrm{crit}}\) and the conversion **before** evaluation, through the review chain. The N4-a family is separate from the single real-data primary test and from all descriptive reports (§7).

**N4-a's real-data scope (rewritten in v1.4 — GPT v1.3 adjudication, blocking finding 3).** N4-a is a **synthetic control**: its generating process deliberately contains no \(C_D\) pathway, and repeated positive exceedances under that known truth model characterize the analysis procedure's behaviour — its false-positive behaviour, leakage sensitivity, and misspecification sensitivity — **within the declared control pipeline**. Four predeclared statements, kept distinct so they cannot be confused:
1. **N4-a evaluates the behaviour of the analysis procedure under its specified synthetic null.** It is a calibration audit of the declared null machinery — nothing more.
2. **The real-data permutation test tests the declared conditional exchangeability null** (\(H_0^{\mathrm{perm}}\), §5.2) on the observed sample.
3. **A positive real-data result does not, by itself, establish leakage or model misspecification.** On real data the truth is unknown; an upper-tail rejection may reflect direct concentration information, the N3 parent-quality proxy, N4-b unresolved-placement proxying, or a pathway outside the declared catalogue (§5.6, V6). (A real-data rejection is "inferentially supported beyond finite-sample null variability" in the Amendment D sense — a statement about the evidence relative to the predeclared null variability, not a classification of its cause.)
4. **The N4-a calibration result cannot identify the cause of an observed real-data association.** It calibrates the procedure; it does not classify a real-data finding.
On real data \(\theta_g\) and \(\xi_v\) are latent; nothing on real data is classified as leakage or model misspecification on the strength of the N4-a control, and channel attribution remains governed by §5.6 and §§9–10.

**(vii) Replication availability and the family decision (new in v1.3; GPT v1.2 adjudication, blocking finding 4).** Each of the \(N_{\mathrm{rep}} = 100\) replications per predeclared \(\lambda\) is subject to the §5.7 V5 availability conditions on its own dataset. Declared handling: (a) **no replacement after generation begins** — replications are generated and processed in a predeclared seeded order; a replication whose dataset is unavailable (design-audit failure, fitting failure beyond the §4 item 3 deterministic rule, or enumeration/sampling infeasibility) is **recorded with its reason and not replaced**; (b) if **any** replication for a predeclared \(\lambda\) is unavailable, the exceedance count \(K_\lambda\) is **not computed** — in particular, \(K\) is never calculated from fewer than 100 successful replications with the original \(\mathrm{Bin}(100, 0.025)\) calibration — and the \(\lambda\)-specific exceedance test is **declared unavailable**; (c) if any of the three \(\lambda\)-specific tests is unavailable, the **Holm family decision cannot be completed as predeclared** and is declared unavailable: the completed members are reported as incomplete-family diagnostics only, never as confirmatory leakage/misspecification declarations; (d) the confirmatory N4-a declaration is made **only when all 100 replications complete for every predeclared \(\lambda\) value and all three \(\lambda\)-specific tests and the Holm procedure complete**. Unavailability counts, per-replication reasons, and the resulting family status are reported (§11(g)).

## 9. N2 integration: exact per-cell regime audit

Before any N2 scoring: per eligible cell, the strong-affinity audit of Rev 1.4 §7.3 determines \(\Psi_q(k,\ell;\Delta^F,\Delta^U)\) and the regime by **exact comparisons on unrounded \(\Phi\) values** — rounded or floating-point-comparison values are never used for regime determination. Ties (\(|\Psi|>1\)) are reported as ties; regime (D) means \(M\) constant on \(\Psi\), regime (T) means \(M\) varies on \(\Psi\); the regime is determined by the audit, never by tie presence, dimensionality, or discreteness (Amendment B vocabulary, binding here). Regime-(D) cells are expected to carry zero within-cell information under N2; any persistent variation on regime-(T) cells is tie-dependent and flagged as such. The audit table (cell, \(\Psi\), tie status, regime) is reported.

## 10. N3 reading and the proxy-pathway check (Rev 1.4 §7.2, §9 item 5)

1. **Reading distinctions (binding, restated):** a positive B1 gain under N3 is reported as **consistent with latent parent-quality prediction** through the \(\theta_g\) channel; it must not be described as evidence of a direct concentration mechanism; it must not be scored as an automatic failure of the predictive target; and it is not automatically a false positive. N3 robustness is a precondition for interpreting a positive real-data B1 result.
2. **Proxy-pathway check (GPT, 2026-11-14; weighting restructured in v1.3 per GPT's v1.2 adjudication, correction a).** Under declared-DGP evaluation, where \(\theta_g\) is generated and therefore known: **Primary summary — parent-level correlation.** Within each eligible cell, compute the Pearson correlation between parent-mean \(C_D\) (the mean of \(C_D\) over the cell's scored versions of each contributing parent) and \(\theta_g\) across the cell's contributing parents. The primary summary is the unweighted mean of these within-cell parent-level correlations over eligible cells (equal weight per cell), with every per-cell value listed. **Per-cell reporting (v1.4 — GPT v1.3 adjudication, item 5b):** for every eligible cell the report lists the contributing-parent count, the sample variance of the parent-mean \(C_D\) values, the sample variance of the corresponding \(\theta_g\) values, and the Pearson correlation **or the explicit mark "undefined" with its reason** (fewer than two contributing parents, or zero variance of either variable). The primary summary is computed over the eligible cells **with a defined correlation**; cells with an undefined correlation are **excluded from that average by this predeclared rule and reported with their reasons** — never silently omitted, imputed, or replaced. Cells with exactly two contributing parents are included when their correlation is defined and are reported with their fragility noted (the correlation rests on two points). **Labelled secondary — version-level correlation.** Within each eligible cell with \(D_c \ge 2\), the Pearson correlation between version-level \(C_D(v)\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{par}(v)}\) over the cell's scored versions — **explicitly labelled as a version-weighted descriptive statistic, not a parent-independent sample**, since \(\theta_g\) is repeated across all versions of each parent (GPT, 2026-11-14). This check shows whether the intended proxy pathway actually operated, rather than merely that it was possible. On real data \(\theta_g\) is latent and the check is not computable; the §10 item 1 reading rule governs real data instead.
